> **Title card：** Sequel · UTTU Scouting Trip

Crossing most of that vast building—the long corridors, the broad staircases, the open ground beneath its fallen dome—left me nearly spent. You can probably guess how I felt. I checked my notebook of hurried sketches one last time and told myself: "Just a little further—that tavern drink the locals talked about is waiting for you."<br>Unsurprisingly, the "neighbors" were at the tavern too. The old woman behind the bar mixed me a quick drink. Beside me sat a man in an old postal uniform, blank-faced, his shoulders worn pale. A ragged, half-transparent canvas mailbag rested on his lap. It seemed I had a "ghost" for company.<br>"You're welcome to move seats if he makes you uneasy. Old Michał never listens to anyone anyway," the old woman offered gently.<br>I thanked her but stayed put. It was, after all, a good chance to rest while observing a "ghost." The old postman's shade sat in silence, staring at the grain of the bar counter, as if an after-work vodka should have been waiting there. Not far off, an old woman wrapped in a heavy shawl stood by a table, taking out the same photo—a photo-shaped shade—again and again, turning it over in her hands. I guessed she must have lived alone in some apartment block, in a room that always smelled of coal stove and old cloth.<br>By the time I finished my drink and looked back, a man in work clothes was standing at the center of the empty tavern—I couldn't say when he'd arrived—as if he'd been waiting there for a hundred years, a thousand years. Half his face was burned, and I caught the faint scent of ash coming off him.<br>Something about his face must have drawn me in, because I found myself standing, trying to speak with him.<br>"Sir, if I may ask, what are you doing here?"<br>"Waiting." His answer came out hoarse.<br>"Waiting for whom?"<br>"A woman with eyes as blue as Lake Baikal."<br>"You're hurt, sir."<br>"No ... this isn't a wound."<br>"It's Her ... it's proof that God has forsaken us."<br>He raised a hand to the burn scarring his cheek.<br>"We were promised gold. That year, winter went on forever. I waved her goodbye and headed into the frozen wild.<br>The workers labored on, day and night.<br>Until the black oil erupted from the wells, flames rising and fading in the falling snow."<br>His pupils trembled faintly, a silent fire burning somewhere in him.<br>"Our sky broke open—but no dawn ever came."<br>I think I knew where he'd come from. Everyone in this country knew where he and his scars came from.

When I was still a student, I spent a year traveling, visiting buildings.
