## 3810101 · "Fin-de-Siècle"

> **Subtitle：** From melancholy it came, and to melancholy it returns.

*ring*

**Hannah**：H-Hello?

**Grundriss**：Hannah? Good, you're still home. Where are Mama and Papa?

**Hannah**：They're here, with me. Something's wrong, Ida.

**Hannah**：Strange things keep happening, one after another. People aren't acting like themselves. And the rain ...

**Grundriss**：Hannah, listen to me. Lock yourselves in. Do not leave the house. And whatever you do, don't touch the rain.

**Hannah**：You know something, don't you? That Foundation you work for—what did they say? Is the world ...

... going to end tonight?

**Grundriss**：The world didn't end that night. It only rained.

**Grundriss**：But almost everyone on Earth dissolved in it. None of my family survived.

**Grundriss**：Time rewound to 1996. It doesn't sound that far back, but it changed everything.

"Foundation Headquarters collapsed. Our systems are paralyzed."

"It is with deep regret that we confirm Laplace has lost its director. Monitoring will continue."

"Manus Vindictae will not stop at this. Every necessary measure will be taken."

"Based on available data, we believe this temporal anomaly to be persistent and irreversible."

On the 31st of December, 1999, our lives were changed forever.

> **Title card：** Meeting Room, Foundation

**Lucy**：Recorded anomalies include mass illusions and inverted rainfall, subsequently leading to spatial and temporal reversion. At present, no viable method has been identified to restore our previous time-space continuity.

**Lucy**：For brevity and clarity, this phenomenon will hereinafter be referred to as the "Temporal Anomaly."

**Lucy**：Preliminary data suggests Manus Vindictae may be involved, although their methods remain unknown.

**Lucy**：However, this meeting is not concerned with their intentions or their methods.

**Lucy**：Our focus will be on the LSCC's current progress in mitigating the anomaly and preventing a second occurrence.

Her cold, mechanical voice reverberates around the room.

The young staff members begin whispering among themselves.

**Foundation Staff Member I**：That woman on stage—Madam Lucy. You ever heard of her before?

**Foundation Staff Member II**：Yeah, a little. She was the head of Laplace Third Branch in New York before they transferred her back to headquarters and made her the lead of the Temporal Anomaly investigation. I heard their new director's drowning in problems—probably why they put her on stage instead.

**Foundation Staff Member I**：Ah, so she's one of those. Guess Laplace has gone the same way as us. Whoever survives gets the promotion.

**Foundation Staff Member III**：At least they found someone to fill the role. They've got some departments that were completely wiped out.

**Grundriss**：That's enough, Finn.

**Grundriss**：She doesn't look up as she speaks, her pen still gliding across the paper.

**Grundriss**：This is a serious occasion. Every piece of information could be crucial. I want your full attention.

**Foundation Staff Member III**：Crucial how? Does any of this even matter—

**Foundation Staff Member III**：When this rain could just come again and wipe us all out?

**Grundriss**：Listen first. Whine later.

**Foundation Staff Member III**：Alright.

He shrugs and turns his attention back to the speech.

**Lucy**：Based on Admiral Igor's report from the front and the Kirlian images we have gathered ...

**Lucy**：We speculate that there are additional locations immune to the anomaly's effects.

**All**：...

**Grundriss**：Immune to the anomaly?

**Lucy**：It is currently unclear whether the Temporal Anomaly always results in time reversal. Until we have a more comprehensive understanding of the sites in question, they will be collectively referred to as "Points Irreversible."

**Lucy**：Our utmost priority must now be to establish common ground for future collaboration and operations.

**Lucy**：So, I will put aside the mathematical deductions and proceed to our conclusion.

She flips to the next slide.

It's a map of the world scattered with several haloed blue lights.

**Lucy**：In summary, our results have identified the following points marked here by blue lights.

**Lucy**：Each one is analyzed to be potentially protected against the recent anomaly.

**Foundation Staff Member I**：So, they're safe zones? Then, that means ...

They can't help but discuss the implications.

The lights are soothing, as if they were meant to wake the attendees from a lengthy nightmare.

**Foundation Staff Member II**：We can relocate our data center, biobank, and armaments to these places. We'd be able to preserve our critical assets.

**Red-Haired Lady**：This is about more than just storage sites.

A woman rises from her seat. The movement lacks any kind of dramatic flair, but the room falls silent nonetheless.

**Red-Haired Lady**：Pardon the interruption. Before we proceed further, I'd like to bring another possibility to the table.

**Kenna**：My name is Kenna. I'm a recent transfer to the Engineering Department at headquarters. I was previously at our Glasgow office.

**Kenna**：I have been acting as the primary point of contact between Laplace and the Engineering Department as we study the feasibility of mitigating and preventing further instances of this Temporal Anomaly.

With her introduction out of the way, she nods at Lucy and gets straight to the point.

**Kenna**：Madam Lucy and I agree. Our primary concern is not the cause of the anomaly but what measures we can take to withstand or stop it.

**Kenna**：If these Points Irreversible prove reliable, they could offer more than just a safe zone for a Foundation database or arsenal.

**Kenna**：They could become something more permanent. An office, say, or even a full branch site.

The word "branch" immediately incites debate among the staff members.

**Foundation Staff Member III**：Sounds nice, sure, but isn't it too soon to start talking about branching out? Headquarters is still all kinds of lopsided.

**Foundation Staff Member II**：It would be a significant step forward.

Kenna scans the room as she waits for the discussion to calm down, then continues.

**Kenna**：It is only a possibility for now, but one we're pursuing seriously. The establishment of a new office is top of our agenda, with Mr. Irinei as its chief director. I will be working alongside him from this day forward.

**Kenna**：The Engineering Department will provide a comprehensive plan, from field exploration and site selection to a full safety evaluation.

**Irinei**：...

Irinei, seated at the center of the conference room, gives a nod.

**Irinei**：As we speak, our team is assessing all potential factors that could impact this project. In the meantime, we will continue to deploy field agents to investigate the areas in question.

**Kenna**：That is correct.

**Kenna**：After much deliberation, and with the approval of the president, the first location we've selected for investigation is—

**Kenna**：Nowa Miedź, our best candidate for a Point Irreversible in Eastern Europe.

Everyone's eyes lock onto the sole red dot on the map.

**Grundriss**：Nowa Miedź ...

The dot is located to the right of the continent's center.

It pulses like a lonesome little heart.

**Kenna**：A small city, known for its factories, canals, and vodka.

She turns her eyes from the thick packet of documents in her hands to the junior staff members by the wall.

**Kenna**：Ms. Bkornblume is currently operating in the area and has already made progress. As per her request, we are planning to assign a small team of architectural specialists to join her in the field.

**Foundation Staff Member III**：So that's why we're here.

**Grundriss**：Sorry, ma'am, could you clarify the role architecture is playing in this investigation?

**Kenna**：It may sound a bit far-fetched, but according to Ms. Bkornblume—

She pauses.

**Kenna**：The immunity zone in Nowa Miedź appears to have a close connection with a particular building.

**Committee Member I**：A building?

**Kenna**：Correct. Unfortunately, no credible records of its construction date or architect remain, and what little information we have left in the headquarters' archive hasn't given us anything of use.

**Kenna**：Furthermore, Ms. Bkornblume has reported multiple irregularities within the structure itself.

**Kenna**：We believe these irregularities may be tied to the building's immunity.

> **Title card：** Hallway, Foundation

**Irinei**：Grundriss.

With the meeting concluded, Irinei finds his subordinate.

**Grundriss**：Mr. Irinei.

**Irinei**：You don't look too pleased about your assignment.

**Grundriss**：That building in Nowa Miedź?

**Grundriss**：With respect, no.

She cuts to the chase.

**Grundriss**：The prevailing theory in the office is that the Temporal Anomaly wasn't just a bizarre act of nature. It was the work of Manus Vindictae.

**Grundriss**：If that's true, then we're looking in the wrong direction. The anomaly isn't the priority. Its creator is.

**Grundriss**：What we need are countermeasures, intelligence, weapons, not architects and land surveyors.

**Grundriss**：We could search every inch of the world and find a hundred safe zones or more, but it'll mean nothing if no one stops Manus Vindictae from coming after us.

**Irinei**：In that we're agreed.

**Irinei**：But Kenna sees things differently. As she put it, "It's not about the building itself. It's about what it could mean for us."

**Irinei**：She believes we must preserve and bolster what we have left. To her, that means securing these Points Irreversible, gathering intelligence, and even establishing more observatories and regional branches.

**Grundriss**：I do see her reasoning. But I'm also worried we might not last long enough to even set those facilities up.

**Grundriss**：We've seen what this storm can do—three years gone in a moment. Who knows what could happen if it strikes again?

**Grundriss**：What good is gathering intelligence from the world as it is now if we're all going to be wiped out again?

Neither one of them says anything further.

The halls are far quieter than they used to be, with only the occasional staffer flitting through them like a ghost.

**Grundriss**：Pardon the negativity, sir. I'm frustrated. We all are. We've lost friends, family, our entire lives to this anomaly.

**Grundriss**：But, grumble as I might, I will do my job.

**Irinei**：I never doubted you. Look on the bright side. You'll be partnering with Cornerstone. You've worked together before, if I recall.

**Irinei**：Nowa Miedź is her hometown. Perhaps she'll make the place feel a little less strange for you.

**Grundriss**：Cornerstone ...

The corner of her lip twitches.

**Grundriss**：I need not remind you, sir. Our last collaboration ended in a quite public argument over the facade material for the London branch. You were there.

**Irinei**：But then you've also delivered many successful projects together, haven't you?

**Irinei**：That's all just part of the job.

**Grundriss**：Of course, sir.

**Irinei**：You aren't expected to figure this all out yourself.

**Irinei**：If there's one thing our teams are aligned on, it's that we need information.

**Irinei**：This investigation could be the first step toward forming concrete plans, for the next anomaly, or even to strike back at Manus Vindictae.

**Irinei**：That said, we have our own agendas. You are answerable only to me, not her.

**Irinei**：You've been assigned as Cornerstone's assistant, yes, but in title only.

He pauses. The meaning behind his words is already self-evident.

**Grundriss**：Understood.

**Irinei**：Before you go, there's something I'd prefer not to say in front of the others.

**Irinei**：You heard what Kenna said about those "irregularities."

**Grundriss**：I did. But what kind of irregularities? What risk level are we dealing with? Could they pose a threat?

**Irinei**：Here. This is Ms. Bkornblume's latest report. It's best to find your answers there.

Grundriss accepts the papers.

**Grundriss**：Appreciated, sir. In all the years I've known her, Bkornblume has never steered me wrong.

She flips through the pages, all detailed records of the Nowa Miedź residents' daily lives.

**Grundriss**：Ah, found it. "Irregularities Overview."

**Grundriss**：Missing persons ...

**Grundriss**：Reports of unusual sounds ... puppets ...

**Grundriss**：And a ghost-like female figure?

**Grundriss**：...

A slight frown crosses her face as she tucks the report away.

**Grundriss**：I'm afraid I'll need more than witness accounts and hearsay to properly prepare, sir.

**Irinei**：Bkornblume will brief you in detail when you arrive.

He gives Grundriss an encouraging pat on the shoulder.

**Irinei**：It's time. Go to this Point Irreversible, evaluate it for construction, and report back to me.

**Irinei**：Stick to the job. No getting involved in unnecessary trouble.

**Irinei**：You've always had a good head on your shoulders. I trust you'll follow it for this task, too.

## 3810102 · Eastward

> **Subtitle：** To remember where you come from is part of where you're going.

**Grundriss**：The plan was to meet Cornerstone at this station so you could give us both the route to the building.

**Grundriss**：But, of course, someone didn't show. Shall we go ahead without her?

**Bkornblume**：Certainly. Now, if you head north on Górnicza Street and turn east ...

Dust drifts through the single ray of sunlight that's made its way into the room.

Only the rhythmic click-clacking of the telegraph machine on the table interrupts her brief pause.

**Bkornblume**：Uh, first, you are able to use cardinal directions, are you not? Not to imply any negative assumptions. It's just easier if I can direct you that way.

**Bkornblume**：My apologies. Reception is unstable here. I suspect due to the building's structure—

**Bkornblume**：Hello? Grundriss? Can you hear me?

**Bkornblume**：...

Unfortunately, the only reply she receives is static.

**Bkornblume**：Oh! That's not good.

> **Title card：** Street, Nowa Miedź

**Grundriss**：Bkornblume?

The call has been cut short. Grundriss glares at her communicator and gives it a shake.

Yet it remains silent.

**Grundriss**：Well then.

**Grundriss**：And, of course, the file didn't include exact coordinates. I can't just be expected to wander the streets blindly.

**Grundriss**：Looks like I'll have to rely on Cornerstone, assuming she knows the way. Did anyone even check in on her? She's probably collapsed in some office in Brussels after working herself to the bone again.

She stares ahead for a moment, then takes out her trusty notebook and pen.

18. April 1996.

Cloudy.

Day three in Nowa Miedź.

The river, the roads, the fog ... Maybe it's only a reflection of my mood, but this whole place seems grim.

And I'm about to head to a presumably equally grim building to meet Bkornblume.

**Drunkard**：*exhale*

There's a man sprawled out across the train station's lone intact bench. A bottle rolls over from his direction.

**Grundriss**：I think this is your drink, sir.

**Drunkard**：Thanks.

He takes a swig.

Grundriss can't help but note how young he looks.

**Grundriss**：You know it is nine in the morning, right?

Just a tad early for alcohol.

**Drunkard**：Nine? Already?

**Drunkard**：Nine in the morning, nine at night—what's the difference? As long as there's Żubrówka and a ballroom or two open.

**Grundriss**：...

**Drunkard**：Nine in the morning. Right, time to go home.

He stands and stretches, as if bidding farewell to his night, or perhaps even day, of drinking.

Behind him stand rows of abandoned buildings, their pillars and frames exposed like the skeletons of some great rotting beasts.

Bottle of booze in hand, the man stumbles away.

Eventually, his silhouette disappears beyond the horizon.

**Grundriss**：Yet another charming addition to this city.

**Grundriss**：Huh?

It's begun to rain.

The pavement darkens in growing patches as it falls.

**Grundriss**：Drizzling. Just like that day. Or at least, that's what I thought.

**Hannah**：Ida ...

**Hannah**：Is the world ... going to end tonight?

Her voice sounds so distant amid the rainfall.

It's only been a few short months, yet Grundriss already finds herself struggling to picture her face.

**Grundriss**：Hannah ...

Another familiar voice reaches her ears.

**Grundriss**：Hmm? Wait. Is that ...?

White blocks unfold to form a dome above her head, shielding her from the drizzle.

**Grundriss**：Cornerstone?

**Cornerstone**：It's been a while, Grundriss.

**Cornerstone**：Don't stand in the rain. You'll catch—

**Cornerstone**：Ah—Achoo! A cold.

**Cornerstone**：Haha. The Materials Department have outdone themselves with this defective shipment. Reliably unreliable as ever.

**Grundriss**：...

**Grundriss**：Insufficient surface tension, poor choice of connection points, the vaguest idea of a drainage slope rather than anything useful ...

A ruthless evaluation. Cornerstone would expect no less.

**Cornerstone**：Mmm. I agree with every word you said. You know how fiberglass is. We have to work within its limitations.

**Cornerstone**：I just didn't expect it to fail quite so quickly.

**Grundriss**：I was almost worried you'd changed, Ms. Cornerstone.

**Grundriss**：But frankly, it's elegant, like a stork on the wing.

Cornerstone winks.

**Cornerstone**：I knew you'd like it.

**Cornerstone**：Rain's letting up. Shall we?

Her right hand traces an arc in the air, and the white blocks withdraw.

**Grundriss**：Good to know you still remember the way.

**Cornerstone**：At the very least, I used to know. I've been there before, but it was a long time ago.

**Cornerstone**：One of those notorious "haunted" spots—I'm sure you grew up with them too. We played hide-and-seek there when we were little. Scared ourselves for fun.

**Cornerstone**：I haven't been able to reach Ms. Bkornblume. But lucky for us, I brought my own map.

Cornerstone opens her suitcase and pulls out a handful of blueprints, a damaged drawing board, a compass ... and a twig.

**Grundriss**：You always manage to pack half your life into that suitcase, don't you?

**Cornerstone**：Hah. There it is!

**Cornerstone**：Look.

A series of chaotic lines and shapes come together to form an abstract something-or-other.

**Cornerstone**：Ah, ignore the scribble on the side. Sorry, it was a spur-of-the-moment idea.

**Cornerstone**：Just try focusing on the blue lines.

At some point in the process, she conjures a white pedestal. She sits on it, sipping a cup of piping-hot coffee.

**Grundriss**：What are you doing?

**Cornerstone**：It's just coffee, though it might as well be my blood for as much as I drink.

**Cornerstone**：I'm sure you already know that, before this, I was heading up the renovations to the Foundation's headquarters. They're planning to relocate the Committee Building to a cleaner area.

**Cornerstone**：I haven't slept in 48 hours, or at least, it feels that way.

Even through the caffeine, a hollowness seeps into her voice.

**Grundriss**：Tragic. I don't imagine the Foundation will be cutting you any slack now that you're the only architect left.

**Cornerstone**：Your life isn't exactly a walk in the park either, being the only supervising engineer about. I suppose that makes us quite the team, don't you think?

**Grundriss**：That's one way to put it.

**Grundriss**：Where did you even find that coffee?

**Cornerstone**：There's a cafe along the way, run by a local woman and her three lovely girls. They have a dog named Leika. We bonded quickly.

**Grundriss**：So that's what those doodles were about.

**Cornerstone**：Hahaha. Well, I couldn't just pass the place by, now could I? I mean it. If someone ran my blood right now, it'd be twenty percent cappuccino, thirty percent Americano, and the rest cold brew.

Grundriss can certainly believe that, given the darkness under her eyes.

**Grundriss**：...

**Grundriss**：It'll be the end of you one day, just like you'll be the end of me.

She lets out a sigh and turns her attention back to the scrawl Cornerstone has chosen to call a "map."

Lines, stains, and colors meld together, overlapping and then dissociating from one another.

Finally, a clear route reveals itself.

**Grundriss**：Lovely route illustration, by the way.

**Grundriss**：You always manage to rekindle the last vestiges of my faith just before I give up on you entirely.

**Grundriss**：It's to the northeast of the old factory district. As for the exact location ...

She turns herself northeast, one of her pupils warping with shifting patterns to give her a better look.

Amid the fog, the old city lets out a labored breath, and the wind cuts through cracked stone walls.

The cityscape—run-down buildings, shops, and residences alike—gives way to black and white lines.

Her gaze flows through them. A shear wall here, a floor slab there ...

Until—

**Grundriss**：...!

**Grundriss**：There's a structure I can't see through. That has to be it.

**Cornerstone**：Perfect timing! Just finished my last sip.

With the last of her coffee flowing through her veins, Cornerstone's eyes light up.

She leaps to her feet.

**Cornerstone**：Hah. I'm thoroughly recharged and ready for a walk! Northeast, you said?

**Grundriss**：I believe so.

**Grundriss**：I studied the map beforehand. So, I've got a general sense of where we're headed.

**Cornerstone**：Fantastic. You may already know the place better than I do.

**Cornerstone**：I moved overseas with my family when I was eight. My knowledge of things here after that is ...

**Cornerstone**：Limited, at best.

Against the backdrop of gray, her fashionable brown and yellow coat, suitcase, and fine leather boots make her look ...

... quite out of place.

**Grundriss**：That explains the accent. Is there anyone you still—

She stops before the rest of her sentence can spill out.

**Cornerstone**：Ah, I don't mind the question. I checked Ms. Bkornblume's list of survivors. I didn't recognize any of them.

**Cornerstone**：It seems as though all the people I once knew here are gone, or "temporarily out of contact," if we prefer to be optimistic.

**Grundriss**：I'm sorry.

**Cornerstone**：Hey, don't. It's not your fault.

**Cornerstone**：We all know who's responsible, and they'll answer for it. Right?

Cornerstone stretches, picks up her suitcase, and takes a few steps forward.

Her silhouette somehow manages to both mirror and contrast with that of the drunkard earlier.

**Cornerstone**：Good, enough heavy talk. Let's get moving, partner.

**Cornerstone**：If you're interested, I can tell you a few childhood stories on the way.

## 3810103 · Sound of Silence

> **Subtitle：** If you step into silence, you'll hear it.

### Segment 1 · `script 203803`

The two keep a bit of distance as they walk, one staying behind the other.

**Grundriss**：I think the building is just up ahead.

**Grundriss**：Do you remember what it looked like?

**Cornerstone**：...

**Cornerstone**：Silence.

An unexpected answer.

**Grundriss**：Silence? In what way?

**Cornerstone**：I couldn't say exactly. That's just the closest to the feeling that I can get. There was a song, though.

**Grundriss**：What kind of song? Do you remember the lyrics?

**Cornerstone**：...

Cornerstone stares at Grundriss, her lips parted for a long while. But no words come out.

**Cornerstone**：Sorry, it was too long ago.

**Cornerstone**：I only remember it the way I remember this city. Blurred childhood impressions, light and shadow. Nothing solid.

**Cornerstone**：The streets feel familiar, the faces too. But when I look closer, I don't recognize anything.

**Cornerstone**：Here I am, standing right in the middle of it. And yet, it's never felt further away.

**Grundriss**：I get that.

The two fall back into silence.

In the stubborn spring fog, all Cornerstone can make out ahead of her is Grundriss's silhouette, shrinking as she walks further and further into the haze.

**Grundriss**：This is the building I saw.

They come to a halt.

The building sticks out in the fog, as if it were welcoming them.

A great, primal force lurks inside.

Like a beast slumbering within a cave.

**Cornerstone**：Architecture is more than just finding a harmony between beauty and utility.

**Cornerstone**：Buildings are what ground us—remind us that we're not just a bunch of abstract thoughts bouncing around in our own heads. In the spaces they create, we become aware of our own existence. But here, I feel exclusion.

No cracks, no colors; all subjectivity has been firmly shut out.

What remains is but a single reality—a looming structure and the two tiny figures at its feet.

**Grundriss**：Let's go in first, shall we?

**???**：'Twas night, and three puppets sang.

Distant footsteps echo into the depths of the building.

**Honza**：Hmm. Breathy-wheezy, Honza hears breathing.

**Honza**：Everecho! Come and look-see.

**Cornerstone**：Take note of these shapes. Triangles, rectangles, circles ... used everywhere. Interesting choice.

**Cornerstone**：Are you familiar with Platonic Forms, Grundriss?

**Grundriss**：Not really. Not interested, either.

**Grundriss**：The reception here is hopeless. Still no connection to Bkornblume.

**Honza**：One, two, three, shadows slip into the mortuary!

**Honza**：Four, five, six, who will shut their eyes and lie there fixed?

**Everecho**：Bkorn ... Bkornblume ...

**Everecho**：I know her.

**Kenna**：That's good to hear. I do hope you two continue to have a productive working relationship.

**Irinei**：...

All Irinei can do is sigh at the prospect.

**Irinei**：In my view, the Committee's approval to advance the Nowa Miedź project wasn't driven by confidence in its practical value, but simply by a compromise reached over cost considerations.

**Irinei**：The vast majority of our branch members still perished in the second "Storm," an outcome hardly better than the first. That as many survived as they did was simply a stroke of luck.

**Irinei**：And we cannot rely on good fortune forever.

**Irinei**：The Foundation's continued operation must depend on a more comprehensive strategy—controllable, judicious—not happenstance like in Nowa Miedź.

**Kenna**：I'd hardly call what they created there "happenstance."

**Kenna**：Judicious and controlled strategy, in my opinion, has rarely produced any great wonders. Certainly nothing like what they've accomplished.

**Irinei**：Nonetheless, you must consider our circumstances, Ms. Kenna.

**Irinei**：Don't mistake me for some old fogey stuck in his ways. I simply learned early on not to build castles in the air.

**Kenna**：I see. Well, I best be off. Best of luck with everything.

The two go their separate ways.

One walks into the shadows at the hallway's end.

The other, into the light pouring in through the windows.

A perfectly sunny and perfectly ordinary day.

One year later

**Grundriss**：3. April 1987.

Sunny.

**Grundriss**：Back in Nowa Miedź with Ms. Cornerstone for a follow-up survey on the new office.

**Grundriss**：It's been so long since our last visit. I'm sure the city has seen all kinds of changes.

> **Title card：** Grundriss's Old Home

**Grundriss**：After this, I suppose we can say that the Nowa Miedź project has well and truly come to an end.

She closes her notebook and sets it on the desk.

It's been tidied up. Only a few worn notebooks remain, stacked on the side.

**Grundriss**：Nowa Miedź is the first city in the East that I've visited as an adult. The friends I made here, the breathtaking lights I saw ... I'll never forget them.

A youthful smile tugs at the corner of her lips.

**Grundriss**：That's all I wanted to share with you today, Grandpa. I have a feeling that everything will only get better from here on out.

**Grundriss**：For Nowa Miedź, too.

**Grundriss**：...

She finds herself on those same cheerless streets, feeling the same wind swing those same faded shop signs.

Same as always.

**Cornerstone**：...

**Cornerstone**：It's almost shocking how little these streets have changed, isn't it? But I do feel something new has quietly taken root in the heart of this city.

**Olga**：Now, now. It may not seem like much, but life here is better than it was before.

**Olga**：Big changes take time to reach a small city like ours. That's all.

**Olga**：We're not in any hurry.

Beneath the gray skies above, Olga stands as a part of the city—an unwavering fixture of it.

Just like the flower patterns on her headscarf, faded from years of washes yet still clinging on. Both are a rare source of warmth among the cool concrete.

**Grundriss**：Hmm.

**Olga**：Come now, dear. Chin up, eh?

**Olga**：Let me take you to the office. That ought to cheer you up.

> **Title card：** Hall, New Office

**Grundriss**：Hmm.

The cold surfaces, pillars, and floors haven't changed.

Even the patterns on the wall tiles are identical.

**Grundriss**：For a moment, I thought I was back at headquarters.

**Grundriss**：Feels like just another day at work. Not exactly cheerful.

**Cornerstone**：It's a perfect example of cost control through template application.

**Cornerstone**：I would've been a little more creative with the look if budget were no object. Unfortunately, it very much was.

**Olga**：When the office first opened, the area around it was still mostly barren.

**Olga**：But they hired some locals to help out with things like guarding the gate, cleaning the rooms, and moving cargo.

**Olga**：Jasio and the others worked here for a while too. But over time, they all went their own ways.

**Olga**：Then the paved roads came in, and long benches were set up around the building.

Cornerstone and Grundriss can't help but be swept up in the nostalgia of her low, slow speech and gentle eyes.

**Olga**：You see, I think that's how Nowa Miedź is changing little by little.

**Grundriss**：And you all changed the Foundation too, in a similarly subtle way.

The sketches, erased and redrawn over and over again beneath neon lights ...

The dust, stones, and labor-worn hands ...

All of it, the good and the bad, pulled that dark and dreary day into the light.

**Cornerstone**：...

**Grundriss**：What? Something cross your mind?

**Cornerstone**：Just a memory. It was right around here that I first met our winsome girl.

She makes an awkward gesture as the memory replays in her mind.

**Cornerstone**：I remember how the light fell across her face, splitting it into two contrasting halves.

**Cornerstone**：And then she sang a song.

**Grundriss**：Mother's Departure, right?

**Cornerstone**：So you remember too. Hah. And here I thought I was the sentimental fool.

**Grundriss**：Hm. She isn't someone you easily forget.

The agreement is left unspoken.

**Cornerstone**：If you don't mind, I think I'd like to check the atrium on my own.

**Olga**：Of course, dear. It's been kept just as you asked.

**Cornerstone**：Thank you.

> **Title card：** Atrium, New Office

The atrium houses a familiar tranquility.

Any clamor has long since left this place.

Cornerstone looks up. The light pouring down from above flows between her and the concrete walls.

**Róża**：Stasia! Hurry up!

**Olga**：The building will remember.

**Everecho**：Don't you remember them? There used to be so many daisies here.

Memories manifest in the light.

Commotion and arguments yield to quiet remembrance.

**Cornerstone**：I see it now.

**Cornerstone**：You never sought anything from the future; you only wanted a shelter for the past.

**Cornerstone**：That is the truest will of your being: to let the echo of existence itself resound within you.

Memory after memory resurfaces. It's enough to leave Cornerstone weary.

She leans against one of its pristine gray walls and closes her eyes.

And in the tranquility, her mind finally finds respite.

...

♫ I wander every distant sod ♫

**Cornerstone**：...!

**Cornerstone**：What is that?

She pulls away from the wall and follows the song to its source.

And she sees it—a little puppet wearing a white smock.

He's lying in a patch of daisies, gazing up at Cornerstone.

**Cornerstone**：Honza?!

**Cornerstone**：If you're here, then that must mean ...

A breeze rustles the flowers, setting light and shadow dancing.

After all, the past can never truly fade, for its song echoes into eternity.

As surely as light casts a shadow.

♫ You knew it true, it's me, my dear ♫
