## 90001601 · Easter (1991-3-31)

**Bkornblume:** "Good evening, friends. You're listening to Abendmuse Radio—your companion beneath Berlin's starry sky."

**Bkornblume:** "I'm Cornflower, your host for tonight's call-in program. Long time no hear. Hope you didn't miss me too much."

**Bkornblume:** "We've talked about family before, and love as well. So tonight, why don't we talk about friendship?"

**Bkornblume:** "That's right. Tonight's call-in topic is 'Best Friends.' If you have a story to share, call us at 01234560."

**Bkornblume:** "Make sure you remember our frequency, so you'll never lose your way back to us!"

The broadcast switches to a musical interlude.

Calls pour in, and the operator gets to work finding their story of the night.

> **Choice 1：** Connect to caller

> **Choice 2：** Do nothing

Before long, Bkornblume is passed a Note containing the caller's basic information.

After a quick once-over, she signals "OK" to the operator.

She clenches and unclenches her fist beneath the desk, steadies her voice, and connects the call.

**Bkornblume:** "Thank you for tuning back in, everyone. Now, let's hear tonight's story. This one is from a caller named Lea. Welcome, Lea. Are you there?"

What she gets isn't the usual greeting but a burst of deafening static.

**Bkornblume:** "Sorry about that. Let's wait a moment. Lea, could you say that again?"

Fortunately, the static lets up.

**Lea:** "Hello, Ms. Cornflower. C-Can you ... hear me clearly on your end?"

The faint tremor in Lea's voice doesn't go unnoticed.

**Bkornblume:** "Loud and clear! And just 'Cornflower' will do. No need for formalities here. Just think of this as a late-night chat between friends."

**Lea:** "O-Okay. I've been a faithful listener of yours for-for a long time. Your voice is so soothing."

**Bkornblume:** "So, I suppose you could say I'm a 'best friend' you've never met?"

**Lea:** "A-Absolutely!"

Bkornblume lets out a well-timed laugh, and the tension melts away.

**Bkornblume:** "I'm honored, Lea! Now then, who's the other friend you wanted to tell us about tonight?"

**Lea:** "Umm. His name's Simon. He was my 'best friend' when I was in secondary school."

**Lea:** "I've never actually met him, though. We were pen pals."

**Lea:** "I didn't have many friends back then, and to my teachers, I was just a quiet girl who did well in class. But secretly, I was really into the alternative scene."

**Lea:** "That's how I made some pen pals. We'd write about graffiti, techno music ... I could be myself with them, you know? I was so, so happy every time I sent off a letter and even happier when I got one back!"

Lea's voice brims with sweet nostalgia.

**Lea:** "Everyone should try it out if they get the chance! I'm sure it's a lot more convenient these days, too!"

Bkornblume senses an opportunity to explore the topic further.

**Bkornblume:** "What would you say was difficult about it back then, Lea?"

**Lea:** "Well, the trouble was, my pen pals were all from other countries, like Hungary and Austria."

**Lea:** "Simon was a special case, though. We both lived in Berlin, but I was in the East, and he was in the West."

Bkornblume's eyes narrow, but she doesn't interrupt her guest, even as the caller's voice falters.

**Lea:** "But that didn't stop us from getting along like a house on fire. We wrote endless letters to each other. He was studying at university then, so he just loved playing the part of the 'worldly senior'!"

**Lea:** "He didn't just share music. He'd talk about all kinds of things—complaints about professors grading finals too harshly and unreliable group project partners ... that sort of stuff."

**Lea:** "And he'd grandly say, 'You'll understand once you get to university.' Haha!"

**Lea:** "We also mailed each other presents for various holidays. I'd make crocheted hats for him with my mother."

**Lea:** "And he'd mail back pretty candies, always making sure to emphasize that he bought them with his own hard-earned cash!"

**Lea:** "But then ... \*sigh\* ..."

**Bkornblume:** "Feel free to tell us more if you'd like. No pressure."

The pause lasts a moment longer. Then, she continues.

**Lea:** "Remember how I said my grades were pretty good at the time?"

**Bkornblume:** "I do."

**Lea:** "Then I'm sure you understand that a student's marks don't just plummet out of nowhere."

Bkornblume purses her lips as memories of past assignments resurface. This won't be good.

**Lea:** "It wasn't like I was distracted during class. I was studying as hard as ever. But my assessments kept coming back with a big red 'Failed' or even the occasional 'Terrible' written on them."

**Lea:** "After this happened a few times, I decided to look over my work, and you know what I realized? The problem wasn't me! Something, or someone, was sabotaging me!"

Her words are flying out at a rapid-fire pace.

In that moment, Bkornblume realizes her worst suspicion has been confirmed. But she knows all too well that any words of comfort will ring hollow.

And so she simply smooths out the note with Lea's information on it and says nothing.

**Lea:** "Once I noticed that, the signs were clear as day, and soon, I realized more and more of my letters received no reply."

**Lea:** "And that was when I really understood what was happening."

**Lea:** "After one sleepless night, I just stopped writing. I never spoke to my pen pals again."

**Lea:** "And just like that, my grades went back to normal, haha."

**Lea:** "Once I got to university myself, I saw just how strict professors could be, and I certainly had my fair share of unreliable group project members. As you do, I made new friends and lost touch with the old ones."

**Lea:** "That's how it is. For better or for worse, nothing in this world lasts forever."

**Bkornblume:** "Perhaps growing older means learning to accept change, or, more accurately, learning to grin and bear it."

**Lea:** "But, sometimes, I can't help but think of my old pen pals, especially Simon. We were so naive back then. We'd promised to keep writing until we couldn't hold a pen anymore, but, well, you've seen how that turned out."

**Bkornblume:** "Would you consider writing to them again?"

**Lea:** "Absolutely! I've given it some thought and realized that maybe growing older also means being brave enough to be the first to reach out."

**Lea:** "Actually, I already mailed some letters a few days ago!"

The operator waves to Bkornblume and points to his watch. Time to wrap things up.

**Bkornblume:** "Have you received any replies?"

**Lea:** "Not yet. But I don't mind waiting a little longer."

**Bkornblume:** "Learning to reach out, but also learning to be patient. Perhaps that's one of friendship's finest forms."

**Bkornblume:** "Hopefully you'll receive a reply soon. Truly, I wish you all the best."

**Lea:** "Thank you, Cornflower."

**Bkornblume:** "And thank you for sharing your story, Lea. That brings today's program to a close. Thank you all for listening. Until next time!"

With the call ended, Bkornblume lets out a breath. She folds the note with a smile and places it in a box on the desk.

With the thought of her freshly planted cornflowers in her mind, she leaves the studio.

The early spring night is clear and crisp. She picks up her pace, eager to get back to warmth.

#### Notes · Abendmuse Radio

Abendmuse Radio's origins go back to 1964, legendary for supposedly broadcasting continuously for 99 hours. At different times it airs music from around the world, and it has also been home to several live performances. It's been available in most regions since around the time I was born, and "Abendmuse" was a household name back in the 80s, though it was especially popular among younger crowds. But most of the time, I listened by "borrowing" my targets' "ears." One target wanted the radio station's "Music Connoisseur" title so badly that he went to bed at 8 PM every evening, just so he could wake up for the 4 AM "Morning Rock" program to write a review to send in. He helped me keep a healthy routine, that's for certain. Another target would accept nothing but heavy metal, while his neighbor considered himself a staunch "defender" of electronic music. The two once came to blows over it, each mocking the other's "bizarre" taste. Some targets were more open-minded, though, and gave everything a fair listen. One of them was a famous pianist, who took part in panels for programs such as "Weekly International Trends." Quite a few of my favorite bands can be traced back to her recommendations. I think all of them influenced me at least a little. Back then, I sometimes wondered whether quitting my job to become a DJ or late-night radio host might let me experience a different kind of life. And to my surprise, that fantasy has become reality!

**7. April 1991. Rainy.**

I got off work early today, so I tried making myself a proper meal.

I've only just started working part time in the MFC kitchen, and as with every new job, I keep worrying whether I can adapt.

But luckily, I have Lore there.

Hard to believe the old me would've considered her a troublesome target!

Who better to keep a secret than someone who "can't speak," right?

No offense to her, of course. Her being mute has no impact on my respect for her at all. I hope we can become true friends.

After all, secrets are not the currency of friendship. Understanding each other is enough.

The person I used to be had to collect so many people's secrets, all while never letting anyone get close.

Though, it wouldn't be fair to say I had no friends back then.

I suppose my **teacher** sort of counts? Even if it was rather one-sided.

And the squirrel family I often saw on my way to work, the beautiful beech tree on my way home, the saffron crocus field I liked to lie on whenever I felt sad ...

And that special target ... I wonder if she ever saw me as an "invisible friend" she'd never met?

But now that I'm trying all kinds of new jobs, I'll have more opportunities to meet new people.

Apart from MFC, I've been a cashier at a supermarket, sorted letters at the post office, and even worked as a part-time reporter. There have been no shortage of unexpected challenges, of course, but I've also met many kind people.

Even if they're not quite "friends," kindness offered freely is a rarity!

Life is as unpredictable as April weather, but flowers root themselves in the earth and hold tight to their little joys.

I do wonder how my **teacher** is doing ...

**28. August 1991. Clear Skies.**

At the morning shift handover, Lore told me that today marks 242 years since Goethe's birth. I had to admit, I'd only ever heard of him.

Fortunately, Lore gave me a little history lesson. Apparently, Brecht didn't care much for Goethe. She also introduced me to some of his poems—pretty interesting stuff.

The world's changed so much recently. Perhaps nothing can surprise me anymore.

Bkornblume pauses in quiet reflection before picking up her pen again.

Even so, I can't deny that I'm lucky enough to live a comfortable life.

Just like Brecht's poem:  **"It's simply luck

That I've survived so many friends. But last night in a dream

I heard those friends say of me: 'Survival of the fittest.'**"

I owe some of that "luck" to my **teacher**, I think. If my records hadn't been kept safe, I wouldn't have been able to try other work.

But the rest was actual luck, like that time I overheard talk of the bank's upcoming 2:1 exchange rate. I would've wasted so many West Marks on the black market.

I mean, back then, 20 East Marks might only get you 1 West Mark if you were trading under the table!

It wasn't because I was special that I saved all that money.

I just had a little cleverness and skill, and more than enough luck.

I still remember someone at a nearby bank almost fighting a clerk because they'd missed the exchange window.

Thinking back on tonight's show, you see a lot of situations like that of Klaus's parents lately. Just a few days ago, I heard the woman downstairs reminiscing about how things were better "back in my day."

The biology professor upstairs said the same thing. According to him, a professor from the West "swooped in" and "stole" his chance at promotion.

But the chemistry doctoral student across the street says things are better now. He's joined a lab in the West, and next month he'll be representing his professor at an overseas conference.

And Lore—she said she used to have so much trouble finding work because of her disability. But there are many more part-time positions available now, which naturally means more opportunities.

For my part, I'd say the changes are mostly good, but by no means perfect. Like the grocery store where I used to buy pickles—all the imported food on the shelves has completely squeezed out the old brands.

The shopkeeper said I ought to be happy, though, since now I can get marzipan whenever I want.

Only the winds of change are eternal, I suppose. But it's hard for those of us caught within them to tell whether they're harsh or gentle.

We either embrace them or grit our teeth and bear them.

She sighs and closes her diary.

She notices the cicadas outside chittering to the stars and wonders what they might be talking about.

The cornflowers beside her window are in full bloom, their many blossoms coming together in a soft blue cloud.

As always, she sets about giving them a good watering before readying herself for bed.
