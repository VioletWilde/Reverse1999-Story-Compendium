## 1390401 · The Histories

> **Title card：** Garden, Anaktoron

The sun hangs overhead, the garden's trees thick and verdant beneath its blaze. It's the hottest time of year.

The gardener wields his loppers, reaching toward the higher branches.

**Gardener**：Hmm?

A red-haired figure appears atop the tree, startling the old gardener into a few backward steps.

**Hedone**：Stefanos? Haha! Watch yourself!

Hedone leaps down from above in a blast of scorching wind.

She strides straight to the fountain at the garden's center and gulps down a handful of water.

**Hedone**：Phew! What refreshment!

**Gardener**：Lady Hedone! Popping up again out of nowhere ...

He steps closer to his queen and sighs.

**Hedone**：Teleportation is simply the most convenient way to travel, dear Stefanos! A joy granted me by our great isle of Ephaistonos.

**Hedone**：Returning here from the coast-side racetrack takes little more than a snap of the fingers for me.

**Gardener**：The racetrack? Then, I'm certain you won yet again, my lady.

Hedone's mouth curls into a smile.

**Hedone**：Of course I did! Winning simply comes too easily to me. I am nigh on becoming bored with it all.

**Hedone**：How I wish we Kymaions would produce another as mighty as Heracles.

**Gardener**：Ah, I almost forgot. You have a visitor today.

**Hedone**：A visitor? But it is not yet the Open Day for the month. Let them take their problems to the island council.

The Kymaion queen belongs to competition and entertainment. But on Open Day, she fulfills the duties of an ordinary queen by listening to her people and solving their problems.

**Gardener**：Well, the anaktoron has no gate, my lady. The people can enter whenever they please, even when it isn't Open Day.

**Hedone**：Haha, yes, a design yet two thousand years old. An enlightened tyrant always keeps the doors of the anaktoron open!

The gardener just shakes his head. He returns to his spot beneath the tree and lifts the loppers again.

**Gardener**：Anyway, she swears she has something important to ask you. She's been waiting in the audience chamber for an hour now.

The odd behavior piques Hedone's interest.

**Hedone**：Oh? I shall take a look.

**Hedone**：After all, an enlightened tyrant always welcomes her subjects' opinions, no matter when they may be offered.

> **Title card：** Audience Chamber, Anaktoron

Hedone strides into the lavish audience chamber and sees a young girl.

She sits bolt upright on the sofa, arms and legs pulled in tight. Something about her face feels familiar, though Hedone can't quite place it.

**Hedone**：You wished for an audience with me?

The girl jolts. She shoots to her feet and bows so low it looks painful.

**Elma**：Greetings, Lady Hedone! I am Elma, a textual archaeologist. It is an honor to meet you!

**Elma**：I don't often attend athletic competitions, so I've rarely had the chance to see you. You look just as you did at last month's ceremony—powerful, beautiful.

**Hedone**：Hahahaha, no need for pleasantries! Let's get to the point.

Hedone sits down directly across from her, waiting for further explanation.

**Elma**：Ahem, yes, right, well, ever since I graduated from school, I've been doing archaeological work on the volcano, the battle against Typhon from a thousand years ago being the focus of my research.

**Elma**：I've collected and compared all kinds of texts, ballads, and a lucky few relics that have survived! In fact, I just completed a survey expedition on the volcano's slope a few days ago.

**Hedone**：Typhon's awakening is imminent. I have already issued an order prohibiting anyone from approaching the volcano.

Hedone's rebuttal silences the scholar again, and she rubs the back of her head, embarrassed.

**Elma**：Of course, my lady! I would never violate your words!

**Elma**：That order was formally issued three days ago, whereas my expedition ended last week, I swear!

She hurries to pull a long, narrow box from her bag and flips open the clasps.

Inside the box is a weathered bronze sword. Its double edge suggests it has been forged in the style of a thousand years ago.

**Elma**：I found this during that expedition. It was wedged in between two rocks.

Hedone takes the ancient sword out of the box and notices faint ancient Greek lettering on the hilt.

**Hedone**：Eurus.

**Elma**：Yes, Eurus, one of the heroes who fought alongside you against Typhon a thousand years ago! I thought you'd be interested to see his sword!

Hedone sets the sword down and shifts into a more comfortable position.

**Hedone**：Such a thing is valuable to me indeed. I'd like to buy it from you. How about ten thousand drachmae?

At this, Elma's mouth drops in shock, and she immediately shakes her head.

**Elma**：Buy it? No, no, no, this is a relic from a glorious age! It was never mine to sell. If you want it, I'd be more than glad to hand it over to you!

**Elma**：All of Ephaistonos was raised on the tales of your heroics. It would be my honor!

She pauses.

**Elma**：But there was something I wanted to ask you about.

Elma digs through the bag beside her, pulling out books of every color and scattering them across the table.

**Elma**：Eurus ... Eurus ...

**Hedone**：You ought to have come prepared. It is ill-mannered to call an audience with your ruler in such a state.

**Elma**：Yes, of course. I'm sorry. It's just ... Ah, here it is!

After a rustle of flipping pages, the scholar locates the passage.

**Elma**：The Hedoneid describes that expedition in detail: "The racing fires devoured Eurus's body, and the shadow of death enveloped him."

**Elma**：But the Chronicle of Heroes mentions "Eurus, swallowed by the tsunami."

**Elma**：Yet in our frescos, he's presented as one of the last few who faced Typhon directly by your side.

**Elma**：Unlike the other heroes, the records about him contradict each other and quite significantly at that.

She steals a testing glance at Hedone.

**Hedone**：So you came directly to me for an answer?

**Elma**：Yes. I suspect the truth lies somewhere between these tales.

**Elma**：A storm blew the expedition beneath the eastern cliffs. The warriors climbed as fire fell from the sky, and in the end, you alone made it down the slope, with the rest dying inside the volcano.

**Elma**：This part is consistent across every account. Only the small details differ.

**Elma**：But if that's the case, his weapon shouldn't have been stuck in the ground along your escape route.

**Elma**：Did he survive, along with you?

Elma grips the hem of her clothes tight, waiting nervously for Hedone's answer.

After a long while, Hedone lets out a soft laugh.

**Hedone**：An interesting guess, but a reincarnator's memory is not much more reliable than any epic. I have long since forgotten plenty of details myself.

**Hedone**：What I do recall is Eurus did die alongside the others in the battle against Typhon upon the volcano.

**Hedone**：When I raised Typhon's eye before our people, no companion stood beside me.

**Hedone**：As for why this sword turned up somewhere it shouldn't have, I couldn't say.

Elma fails to hide the disappointment on her face at this answer.

**Elma**：Oh ... I thought I'd found a major flaw in the record, enough to win next week's paper competition ...

**Elma**：Could it have been blown down there by the wind?

Hedone's attention has already waned, her gaze drifting to the book sitting at the center of the table.

**Hedone**：The Hedoneid, that poet's masterpiece. I haven't seen this edition before. Looks like a recent printing.

**Elma**：Oh, they keep changing the cover design and adding even more footnotes to its pages while they're at it, heh.

Hedone picks up the book filled with tabs and reads the epic's opening line aloud.

**Hedone**："Sing, O goddess, of our heroine Hedone, sing of her wrath. Her wrath, which slew for us the great beast Typhon."

**Hedone**："Let your song begin with that fateful landing, when warriors from afar came ashore and upon those outer islands first laid eyes on the volcano, which would spell both doom and glory."

**Hedone**：...

## 1390402 · Wisdom in Stone

Thick black clouds gather on the far horizon. Red magma spews into the sky, accompanied by a terrible, rumbling thunder.

Hedone stands high on the mountainside, gazing out to the sea and the violent storm which swells it.

**Hedone**：There must be a volcano at its center, but this weather is most unnatural.

**Aganippe**：Hedone!

**Hedone**：What monster might lurk within those swirling tides? A Gorgon, a Harpy, a Cyclops?

**Aganippe**：HEDONE!!

> **Title card：** Highlands, Ephaistonos

Only now does Hedone hear her companion's call from behind her. She turns around, flashing a bold smile.

**Hedone**：Haha, now that, my friend, is a proper war cry!

Aganippe sighs and steps forward to her side.

**Aganippe**：As if I would dare challenge our mightiest warrior.

**Aganippe**：The sacrifice is soon to begin. Why do you tarry here?

**Hedone**：The sacrifice is Eurus's domain. He will handle it.

The sacrificial grounds are directly below where they stand.

Aganippe can see they've already lit the braziers and bound the offering to the post.

**Aganippe**：You are our navigator, Hedone. We are all awaiting your presence.

**Aganippe**：Eurus is no warrior, after all. And there is nothing more respected by the Kymaions than strength.

**Aganippe**：In fact, there has been some displeasure with Eurus as of late. He does not approve of this expedition.

Hedone shrugs and turns her attention back to the storm.

**Hedone**：I am discerning a route for our campaign. With how violent this storm is, it shall tear our ships apart! We require a power to command the wind.

**Aganippe**：That is why we are enacting the sacrifice. We shall need the gods' blessings if we are to survive.

**Hedone**：Hah, the gods! It was a prophecy gleaned from them that led us here. What was it? "An island ringed by fire, a second homeland fated to be. The Kymaions shall rise again, with wealth and power beyond measure."

**Hedone**：And yet we arrive at the sight of a raging storm, an active volcano, and the possibility of a beast within it!

**Hedone**：But I hold no ill will against such twisted jokes.

Hedone narrows her eyes, still focused on the storm.

**Hedone**：Long has it been since I last faced a worthy enemy.

Eurus scoops up a handful of barley flour and lifts it high. The wind carries it away, along with their chanted prayers.

**Eurus**：Time and again, you have watched over us! Time and again, you have granted us glory!

**Aganippe**：If the gods grant their blessing, I wish to join your ranks.

**Hedone**：Oh? Ah, I remember now. You just passed the warrior's spear trial.

**Hedone**：But your only experience is as a market guard. What we mean to do is too dangerous for a new recruit.

**Aganippe**：I am already an adult! You and I were born in the same year!

**Aganippe**：But you have been hunting alone underwater and escorting the family's ships for years.

Aganippe lowers her head slightly, but a hard clap to the back quickly straightens her up.

**Hedone**：Hah! You and I are no fair comparison! There are few who could match a great warrior blessed by the gods themselves!

**Hedone**：But, of course, if you carry the courage to face a monster head-on, who am I to quell it?

**Hedone**：Besides, I owe it to your grandmother, "Nyx" Aganippe. She was a great warrior and an even greater companion. She would be proud of you.

**Aganippe**：Mm, her soul must have ascended to Elysium by now. I will not disgrace her name!

**Aganippe**：Look, the sacrifice shall soon be made.

Eurus lifts the calf's head, exposing its throat.

**Eurus**：Gods of Olympus! We offer you this calf as sacrifice so that you may watch over our warriors, granting them the strength to slay all enemies before them!

**Hedone**：Hmm?

As it is bound, the calf begins to struggle and wail. This is no different from the countless rituals Hedone has already witnessed.

**Aganippe**：I pray the gods answer us. I must prove my strength in battle, Hedone.

**Aganippe**：I must win yet more victories! Just as you have!

Aganippe turns her head to look at her.

**Aganippe**：Hedone, I beseech you ...

Before she can finish, Hedone has leapt off the cliff, bound along the steep rock face, and arrived at the bottom of the ravine.

**Aganippe**：Hedone, where are you—

She trails off as she spots the reason for Hedone's sudden departure.

**Aganippe**：...?!

She turns around to see that the calf on the altar has already broken free of its ropes.

It knocks over Eurus before barreling through the stunned crowd and bolting straight into a nearby forest.

And chasing right behind the calf is a streak of red, like an arrow loosed from a bow.

**Hedone**：Worry not! I shall capture it!

> **Title card：** Forest, Ephaistonos

It's hard to make out the shapes of things under the dim of the thick canopy.

A golden arrow grazes the calf's back and disappears into the darkness.

**Hedone**：Tch, I'm out of practice.

The calf plunges into thick brush and disappears from Hedone's sight.

She pushes through the foliage and eyes its tracks.

**Hedone**：Never before have I seen a sacrifice flee.

**Hedone**：What have we done to make you, no, to make the gods so furious?

The forest falls silent. Hedone draws an arrow and slows her footsteps.

The calf's tracks end in front of a cave.

> **Title card：** Cave, Ephaistonos

A small opening at the top of the cave lets in just enough light for Hedone to make out what's inside.

The white calf lies docile on the ground, and beside it stands a girl barely taller than her waist.

**Hedone**：...!

The girl has long, rose-red hair, like a fiery dawn. Her slightly squinted eyes give her an air of arrogance.

It takes Hedone a moment before she can place her.

Yes, she does indeed know this face. It is herself, as a child.

**???**：Hello.

**Hedone**：Heh, I never said "hello" to anyone in my childhood.

Hedone lowers her bow and arrow, eyeing this other version of herself with interest.

**Hedone**：Which god are you?

She's heard plenty of stories like this. A god takes the form of a mortal to point a hero toward their path or to lead them to their death.

**Hedone**：I did not expect you gods to still have such eccentric interests as this.

The miniature "impostor" watches her calmly.

**???**："All that brings me joy in this dull world is battle. I have no need for wealth. What I lack is amusement."

**???**："Unity is a game only the weak play. A true warrior needs no companions."

**???**："I, Eudora, am a proud, solitary hawk, destined to walk the path of a hero!"

**Hedone**：That is a name I have not heard since I earned my title.

**Hedone**：I hold no embarrassment over my eighth birthday speech.

**???**：It was not my intention to embarrass you. I only wished to endear myself to you.

**???**：This child's form is only a projection. It is not my true self.

**???**：These are words you once said. Arrogant, prideful, ever chasing the thrill of life and death ... that is exactly who you are.

The girl ignores Hedone's silence and gives her a polite nod.

**Volcano**：I am the embodiment of the volcano's will. This island's power rests within me.

**Volcano**：I have watched on as you pursued your "prophecy," crossing an ocean to arrive on these shores. You know well the rites of sacrifice to the gods, and you fight Scyllas with spear and arrow.

**Hedone**：You needn't repeat what I already know.

**Volcano**：But I must disappoint you, for this place will be no settlement for you.

**Volcano**：A great beast with a power to summon storms and fire lies coiled atop this volcano. It unceasingly spews forth fire and flame.

**Hedone**：So, you intend to convince us to flee?

The girl gently strokes the calf. It lets out a contented moo.

**Volcano**：No, Hedone. I intend to strike a deal with you.

**Volcano**：I want you and your people to slay Typhon.

**Hedone**：Typhon?

For an instant, Hedone feels her heart stop.

**Hedone**：The enemy of the gods, the beast of legend?

Her heart pounds hard against her chest, the thrill of excitement nearly drowning out every other thought.

**Volcano**：The very same. A powerful, savage creature. Any challenger is likely to die to its storms, its falling fire, or its poisonous fangs.

Her expression doesn't change at all, as if she's discussing an ordinary grain trade.

**Volcano**：But the rewards I offer are generous.

**Volcano**：Should you prove its victor, you shall become the volcano's new master, and so too will you be granted boundless power.

**Volcano**：I shall forge of you a power greater than Typhon itself.

**Hedone**：That is not enough.

**Volcano**：Not enough?

Hedone hides her excitement to quickly raise the stakes of the deal.

**Hedone**：This shall also be our second homeland, a place to settle and prosper.

**Volcano**：Of course, if that is what you wish.

**Volcano**：Upon these lands, the winner takes all.

There is no haggling at all. Hedone has rarely met a trading partner this agreeable.

**Hedone**：For me, this is a fair deal indeed: high risk, high reward.

**Hedone**：But you gain nothing from it. That usually speaks of trickery.

**Volcano**：There is nothing I need to gain.

**Volcano**：Typhon is far more powerful than any creature or beast upon this island. Its rule here is absolute.

**Volcano**：Its rule has remained unchanged for far too long. I am bored. But a judge cannot join their game. I cannot drive it out myself.

The girl breaks into a grin, a perfectly typical smile.

**Volcano**：You and I are the same. I simply want to enjoy the thrill.

**Hedone**：Hahahahahahahaha!

Hedone bursts into delighted laughter.

**Volcano**：Hahahahaha!

The girl's laughter follows, echoing and colliding with Hedone's within the cave's walls.

**Hedone**：...

**Hedone**：Then there is little reason for me to refuse.

**Volcano**：Do you not fear death?

**Hedone**：You will not see me fail.

**Volcano**：This would be far more than a simple failure.

The girl steps forward and grips her wrist.

**Volcano**：But I care not. Kill it, and bring me its eye.

**Volcano**：As proof of my commitment to our bargain, I shall give you a small gift.

Hedone watches as her golden armor melts and flows, taking on the form of two perfect rings.

**Volcano**：The storms on the periphery are dangerous. The sea beasts know how to navigate them.

The golden rings on her arms gleam brightly. Hedone flicks one, and it releases a few clear, ringing notes.

**Hedone**：This will make a fine addition to my collection.

**Hedone**：What do you say?

She gets no answer.

The cave stands empty save for the sleeping calf.

Hedone bursts out laughing.

**Eurus**：I do not jest, Hedone. You have come of age now.

Hedone waves a hand.

**Hedone**：Once we've conquered this island, I shall lead the fleet onward unto greater and more powerful enemies!

**Hedone**：I shall conquer many bountiful lands, building a coalition of poleis spanning the whole Aegean!

**Hedone**：Even Rome would tremble at the thought!

**Eurus**：Building and holding together an empire so vast takes far more work than mere conquest and killing.

**Eurus**：Balancing expenses, settling disputes, repairing ships, making sacrifices to the gods ...

**Hedone**：Haha, those trifling affairs shall be left to you elders.

**Hedone**：They are no place for a hero!

**Eurus**：...

A cheer rises from the center of the celebrations. The second round of the hunting contest seems to have found its winner.

Aganippe drags a sea beast twice her height ashore and stands proudly beside it.

**Aganippe**：Look, look! My sea beast is even bigger than the one Grandmother caught back in her day!

**Clansman**：Ha, impressive! About the size of Hedone's catch!

**Clansman**：Not bad, little one. Have it roasting soon. I am eager for a taste!

**Eurus**：I heard you have taken Aganippe onto the expedition as well.

**Hedone**：Ha, do not underestimate that one! She can put an arrow through two targets' heads with a single shot!

**Eurus**：The battlefield is no training match. The victor is not decided by skill alone.

**Hedone**：But she has a real talent for hunting and true passion for battle.

Hedone lightly clinks her cup against her friend's.

**Hedone**：I'm off. I shan't have anyone stealing first place from me.

**Hedone**：There are plenty of sea beasts left in these waters. I say another round of hunting might just yield me a bigger catch.

**Eurus**：You two are far too enamored with these competitions.

**Hedone**：Hah! Don't be so dour.

**Hedone**：We haven't had a war since ... that affair. More than a year has passed.

**Hedone**：The warriors are all itching to prove their mettle.

**Hedone**：Competition, battle, passion, revelry ... this is our way of life, is it not?

**Eurus**：That it is, which is exactly why I shall join you in battle.

Eurus drains the wine from his cup.

**Hedone**：Oh? After all those objections, I thought you were firmly resolved to stay out of it!

**Eurus**：...

**Eurus**：You require my arcane skill.

**Eurus**：There is no power more useful against storms than controlling the wind, is there?

**Elma**：I thought you had controlled the wind yourself. The frescos and the epic both say you carry the blessing of Aeolus and Poseidon.

Messy handwriting fills the last of the lines on the page, and Elma quickly turns to a fresh one.

**Hedone**：I never gave you permission to interview and record me.

The scholar, previously lost in her transcribing, is suddenly yanked back to reality.

She sets down her pen, nervously glancing at Hedone a few times, and finally works up the courage to speak.

**Elma**：My apologies, Lady Hedone, but please allow me the honor!

**Elma**：You were there yourself. Your account, commentary, and corrections to the epic are an invaluable historical resource! The opportunity we have is nothing short of a miracle, the kind of opportunity scholars two hundred years ago could only have dreamed of!

**Elma**：With this, I might actually have a shot at winning the paper competition after all!

**Hedone**：And I will not allow my people to become pawns in yours!

**Hedone**：As for power, even without accepting yours, I shall remain the strongest hero in all of Greece.

She puts two fingers to her lips and lets out a sharp whistle. Ceto's back surfaces on the water.

Then, she hears the sound of something cutting through the air behind her.

**Hedone**：...?!

Before she can turn her head, an arrow pierces her left shoulder.

<a id="episode-1390407"></a>
## 1390407 · Phronei Thneta

Hedone spins around and sees Eurus nock another arrow to his bow.

**Eurus**：The winner takes the volcano's power, is that not so?

**Eurus**：If I defeat Hedone, should I not have just as much right to it?

**Volcano**：The rules of the contest do permit it.

The judge steps back, clearing space for the duel.

Hedone snaps off the shaft of the arrow but doesn't force the arrowhead out. The sudden turn of events has struck her as almost laughable.

**Hedone**：Chronios ... Why?

**Eurus**：Why?

**Eurus**：Eudora, why must you always be so arrogant?

**Eurus**：Everything comes so easily to you! You can gain and cast aside whatever you please without a second thought!

Another arrow flies straight at her. Hedone steps aside and catches it in her fist, stopping its flight cold.

The rough wooden shaft tears the skin of her palm, and blood trickles down between her fingers.

**Eurus**：We grew up side by side and endured the same training. And yet you were given so much more than any one of us!

**Hedone**：That was fairly won. I received the most because I was the strongest warrior. You know that!

**Eurus**：Of course I know that! You were born with a god's strength, a true hero. You slay your enemies and hold up their heads to the sound of endless cheers!

**Eurus**：But when the fighting is done, there are repairs to be made—negotiations, trade! This is what truly keeps the Kymaions alive!

Eurus draws a deep breath.

**Eurus**：Of course, a hero like you does none of that. That is the elders' work.

**Hedone**：...

**Eurus**：At least in Greece, being an elder was a somewhat respectable role.

**Eurus**：But once we took to the sea, the fighting only worsened. Against monsters, against Rome ... it never ceased.

**Eurus**：Hedone, the hero protector of our people, naturally earned everyone's respect.

**Eurus**：And the rest of us—we were nothing but the hero's shadow.

**Hedone**：So, you wish to refuse my decision and defeat me?

**Hedone**：But still you took the path of a coward. Do it now. Challenge me openly.

Eurus draws the short sword at his waist.

**Eurus**：Defeat you? Hah. How could I possibly hope to defeat you?

**Eurus**：You know I have never held any interest in sport. My skills of the body are pitifully few.

**Eurus**：But here, among the Kymaions, strength seems to be the only language anyone speaks.

**Eurus**：And it's the one thing I have always lacked. All I want is what's rightfully mine.

**Hedone**：This duel is meaningless.

**Hedone**：Even with my wound, you are no match for me.

**Eurus**：But you already spent your strength defeating Typhon. Besides ...

**Eurus**：I've bested you before.

With a cry of pain, another challenger falls.

**Child**：How did she end up as today's combat instructor?

**Child**：How are any of us supposed to defeat her? She bested a centurion single-handed!

**Eudora**：Haha, how pitiful! Next! Come, then!

The instructor points at the trainee standing in the middle.

**Child**：Chronios, looks like your luck just ran out.

**Chronios**：I wouldn't be so certain.

**Hedone**：Since you are so dissatisfied with my leadership, since you insist so strongly—

**Hedone**：Since you yearn for this power so greatly—

**Hedone**：Let the fight speak for us.

Hedone draws her bow.

She knocks her opponent to the ground with ease.

**Chronios**：*cough*

Chronios raises his shield, catching the spear mid-thrust.

**Eudora**：Hah, still you refuse to yield?

**Eudora**：Let's see how much more you can endure!

**Eurus**：Why is my share always smaller than yours, Hedone?

**Eurus**：Since you would choose to throw it away, give it to me. I need this power!

**Hedone**：Tsk!

**Eudora**：...!

An arrow flies on the wind, grazing Eudora's cheek.

**Child**：That's cheating!

**Eudora**：No. There is no rule against arcane skills.

She releases her hold.

**Eudora**：I yield.

**Eudora**：You have impressed me. I should like to be your friend.

Hedone swings her hand, easily knocking the arrow out of the air behind her.

She flicks the short sword out of Eurus's hand and presses her spear to his throat.

**Hedone**：I shan't be hit by the same move twice.

**Hedone**：Through all these years, I have won countless battles and gained a wealth of experience.

**Hedone**：To me, this is naught but a cheap trick.

**Eurus**：Heh, such a powerful arcane skill! And it ended up in your hands, of all people.

**Eurus**：I do not imagine you'll forgive betrayal.

Hedone falls silent for a moment, then lowers the spear in her hand.

**Hedone**：No. I would not raise a hand against a friend.

**Hedone**：I have lost enough of them for one day.

She holds a hand out to her old companion, but he doesn't take it.

**Eurus**：Hahahaha! I suppose, in the end, I am naught but a mortal.

**Eurus**：Hedone, accept the volcano's power. Become queen of the Kymaions.

**Hedone**：You have misunderstood me.

**Hedone**：What I love is fighting, competition, sailing, trade ... the surge and tumult of excitement! I have no interest in peace.

**Hedone**：Nor do I have any interest in governing or politics. I will not interfere with your work, and I will not accept this power or become queen.

**Eurus**：This is the only way! This is the right choice for the Kymaion people!

**Hedone**：No! This will only please the volcano and stain the glory of our fight! Our people will become playthings, facing disaster time and again!

**Eurus**：Yes, a full thousand years in the future! Standing before Rome as it is now, we have not even ten years to spare.

**Eurus**：The tide is coming in.

Seawater floods over the ground where they'd just been fighting, wave after wave breaking against the ancient rocks.

**Eurus**：Have you already forgotten what happened before we left Greece?

**Hedone**：I have not forgotten.

**Hedone**：I avenged my family through a fair fight, one the gods themselves recognized.

**Eurus**：You, aflame with rage, killed the Roman commander and beat back their assault.

**Eurus**：But in the end, to escape the persecution that followed, we had no choice but to leave our homeland behind.

**Hedone**：That will not happen again. I shall become stronger.

**Eurus**：You can! But what we need is not merely a powerful mortal!

A mouthful of blood gurgles from his throat.

**Hedone**：You ...!

Eurus raises his arm. Black lines have already crept across the surface of his skin.

**Eurus**：Typhon is also a serpent, is it not? I was bitten, back in the crater.

**Eurus**：Today, I shall join them and rest here by their side forever. I accept this as my mortal fate.

**Hedone**：The fighting only hastened the poison's spread. You should never have challenged me!

**Eurus**：I wanted to see whether the Kymaions could survive losing you.

**Eurus**：But heroes always have a fate they cannot escape.

**Hedone**：...

**Eurus**：We need someone who might rival the gods themselves! A guardian, a protector, a power beyond any mortal being!

**Eurus**：Otherwise, the Kymaions will be destroyed by the times, vanquished by the "conqueror of the world." The rest of us are simply too weak to stop it.

**Eurus**：We have endured so much ruin. We have already lost every one of our finest warriors.

**Eurus**：Eudora, do you truly wish to endure more of it? More losses?

**Hedone**：...!

He reaches up and takes hold of Hedone's golden bow.

**Eurus**：Accept this power, a power like Typhon's, a god's power.

**Eurus**：We cannot fight fate, not me, not you.

## 1390408 · Agon of the Ages

The sky has long since cleared, but the sea still shows no sign of anyone's return. The Kymaions gather along the shore, waiting anxiously.

**Clansman**：Why have they not returned?

**Elder**：...

**Elder**：Have patience.

**Clansman**：Look, everyone. What's that?

Far off against the blue-white horizon, an enormous whale comes into view.

And standing on its back is the unmistakable blazing red of their warrior.

**Elder**：It's Hedone! Hedone has returned!

**Clansman**：Hedone has returned!

Ceto swims faster and faster, quickly closing the distance to the outer island's shore.

The Kymaions finally get a clear look at Hedone.

She's covered in wounds, her face streaked with dried blood. Gold runs like liquid across her body, and her cloak billows like fire itself.

**Clansman**：This is nothing short of—

**Clansman**：A goddess, true! Standing before us! Our Pallas, our Cynthia, Mistress of Beasts, Goddess of Victory!

Hedone jumps down from Ceto and raises Typhon's eye high overhead.

**Hedone**：Typhon has fallen!

**Hedone**：We are victorious! And this shall be our home!

The crowd falls into dead silence.

**Clansman**：Hedone!

No one could later say who was first to start chanting Hedone's name, but soon every Kymaion is weeping and cheering.

The crowd surges toward her in a frenzy, more violent than Typhon's hurricane ever was.

**Clansman**：All hail Lady Hedone!

Finally, someone asks the question everyone else has forgotten to ask.

**Clansman**：What of the other warriors?

**Hedone**：They gave their lives for us.

**Hedone**：They fought bravely against the beast Typhon.

**Elder**：Just as the prophecy foretold.

**Hedone**：What prophecy?

**Elder**：Eurus's final one. He asked that we keep it secret until your return. It was his final request of us.

The elder spreads his arms wide, facing the excited crowd.

**Elder**：My friends, it is as the prophecy foretold: our heroes would give their lives for our new home, and Hedone—our hope—would return in triumph, carrying Typhon's eye!

**Elder**：She has already proven her unmatched strength beyond doubt. She will become our new queen!

**Elder**：This is the will of the gods!

**Elder**：All hail our queen, Lady Hedone!

**Tribespeople**：All hail Lady Hedone!

The Kymaions erupt into a roar of celebration.

Someone takes Typhon's eye from her hands, and a golden medal is draped around her neck. The children laugh with delight, tossing flowers and grapes at her feet.

**Hedone**："Someone who might rival the gods" ...

Somewhere in the crowd, she catches sight of that small, red-haired girl once again.

**Volcano**：Congratulations, Hedone.

A frail poet pushes through the crowd, seizing both of Hedone's hands, overcome with emotion.

**Poet**：It is as though we are reliving that day all those many years ago, the day a god first came down to walk among our people!

**Poet**：An epic poem must be written for this. No, a whole fresco must be painted to praise our fierce and mighty queen! Our hero!

**Poet**：Gods above, we Kymaions, we have finally risen again!

**Elder**：The fresco depicting Typhon's eye has just been completed. If you would take a look at this rendering, my lady.

An exquisite copy unfurls before Hedone's eyes.

**Hedone**：Hah, not bad. Everyone looks just as they did.

Five years after the battle against Typhon, peace has settled. The elder finishes his report, bows, and leaves the great hall.

The queen sits back down at her table, ready to unroll the next scroll.

Then, an unfamiliar hand presses down on top of hers.

**Volcano**：These days, this island is nothing but games and competitions, a lesser substitute for war.

**Volcano**："Competition should be a celebration of life and vitality rather than a struggle for survival." I find it hard to imagine you actually agree with that.

**Volcano**：How terribly dull.

**Hedone**：I require neither your agreement nor your opinion.

The girl sits down on the table and picks up a grape from a plate.

**Volcano**：Come now, you spend every day at this desk. You scarcely have time to touch a bow any longer! It has been a full five years since I last saw you draw an arrow on the field.

**Volcano**：Still, I understand your reasoning. The world beyond is at war on every front. Concealing this island and selecting safe shipping routes is no easy task.

**Volcano**：So many of your warriors were killed by Typhon. It will take another decade or so before the new generation can shoulder the responsibility. Only then will you once again have the freedom for contests and sailing.

Hedone signs her name, rolls up the new decree, and stacks it onto the pile of scrolls on the table.

**Volcano**：Still, is this truly the course you mean to take—concealing the fact that a friend once turned his blade on you, ignoring the deaths born of vanity?

**Hedone**：I have concealed nothing.

**Volcano**：True. You simply never chose to explain it. "They are still heroes of the Kymaions."

**Volcano**：Or, as that elder said, "A hero's story must maintain a purity."

**Hedone**：I can bear it alone.

**Hedone**：Their deaths, in the end, were your doing.

The girl turns her head, eyeing Hedone with interest.

**Volcano**：I hold no love or hatred for any of you. I only chase the purest of thrills.

**Volcano**：Watching a new polis rise offers its own kind of excitement.

**Volcano**：Though I am uncertain how long it shall continue to thrill me. A thousand years, perhaps?

Hedone looks up from her paperwork.

**Hedone**：If you are here to provoke me, present yourself before the council. They handle sparring requests over there.

**Hedone**：But if you ever lay a hand on my people, you will find a spear run through your palm. God or no.

The girl shakes her head.

**Volcano**：I came only to bid you farewell. A judge is best suited to the outside of the theater.

**Volcano**：I am your youth, a shadow of your past self, a child in pursuit of nothing but pure strength and thrill.

**Volcano**：But she has been fading further from you. I can no longer maintain this form.

**Hedone**：I have not changed.

**Hedone**：Next time, I shall correct the fate you have twisted out of shape, even if it takes another thousand years.

The girl's form scatters and dissolves in the sunlight.

**Volcano**：A thousand years hence ...

**Volcano**：Will Typhon slay you, or will you slay Typhon?

**Volcano**：Will you lose every one of your friends all over again?

**Volcano**：I look forward to seeing how this thousand-year wager ends.

**Volcano**：If you remember it all by then, reincarnator.

The sunlight has dimmed. It's already dusk.

**Gardener**：Lady Hedone? Lady Hedone?

**Hedone**：Mm ...

**Gardener**：Sorry to wake you, but you have another visitor. He says he wants to discuss tomorrow's prophecy ceremony with you.

Hedone looks out the window. Every stretch of lawn has been trimmed to perfection.

Beyond the lawn, a familiar golden-haired young man waves to her.

**Hedone**：What happened to the scholar?

**Gardener**：Oh, her? She left a while ago.

**Gardener**：Didn't you say you were tired and wanted to rest for a bit? That's why you asked her to leave. You said you'd talk to her again tomorrow.

**Hedone**：Is that so? I guess my head's still a little foggy.

**Gardener**：What were you dreaming about? I thought I heard you shouting.

**Hedone**：Dreaming?

Whatever it was has evaporated like mist.

**Hedone**：I don't recall. I suppose that means I slept well, at least.

Hedone pushes herself up and stretches her joints on the sofa.

**Hedone**：Tell Apollo I'm on my way.

**Hedone**：This time, I shall put a final end to what began all those years ago.
