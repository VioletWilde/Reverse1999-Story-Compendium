## 3882001 · A Hundred Thousand Billion Sketches

<!-- reader-body:start -->
The lecturer dumps a towering pile of manuscripts onto the lectern, making an exaggerated display of wiping the sweat from her brow.

**Cornerstone:** First, let me say how delighted I am to see so many of you still eager and bright-eyed for your final class in Modern Urban Planning.

**Cornerstone:** I realize most of that eagerness is for the coming summer vacation, so I'll keep this brief.

**Cornerstone:** It's been a delight to be your guest lecturer this semester. I'm heartbroken that this will be our last time together as a class.

The young audience responds with a chorus of strange little noises that seem to indicate that they'll miss her too.

**Cornerstone:** Oh, don't be like that.

**Cornerstone:** Once you've finished your studies, should any of you find your way to our firm, I daresay our paths may cross again.

**Cornerstone:** Now then, for the second unfortunate announcement—

**Cornerstone:** Bob, Duvalle, Michelle ...

**Cornerstone:** I'm afraid summer break will have to wait a little longer for you three. Your final assignments were a complete catastrophe. But no need to panic. You still have a chance to resubmit.

**Cornerstone:** Let's start with you, Bob. I'm relieved you're not really responsible for planning Los Angeles's road systems. Limited public transit, long stretches of highway—it'd be a nightmare to get anywhere!

From somewhere below the lectern, a small, frightened voice speaks up.

**Bob:** Madam Cornerstone, I have to disagree. My premise is for a Los Angeles where every American citizen has an automobile of their own.

**Cornerstone:** Is that why you got rid of all the sidewalks and bike lanes?

**Cornerstone:** For the sake of the good citizens of Los Angeles, try again.

**Bob:** Oh, no.

He slumps back into his chair, limp and defeated.

**Cornerstone:** Now on to you, Duvalle. A little praise to begin with, your concept is very imaginative. Floating islands covered in tinfoil, residential blocks under glass domes ...

**Cornerstone:** Unfortunately, your assignment was to develop an urban downtown for the real world, not a science-fiction novel.

**Duvalle:** I based my thesis on a newly discovered substance on the moon, one that can make glass stronger than concrete. It's grounded in practical science.

**Duvalle:** In the not-so-distant future, say, 2062? Our cities could really look like this.

**Cornerstone:** And if I find myself in a city of floating glass in 2062, I promise to hobble over on my walking stick and personally update your grade to a perfect score.

**Cornerstone:** As it stands, however, it's a fail.

**Duvalle:** Oh ...

**Cornerstone:** Now on to you, Michelle.

Cornerstone continues her evaluations, handing each manuscript back to the students, each with a quick offer of praise, critique, or advice, until the room is empty. All except for one student, who lingers staring down at her manuscript.

**Duvalle:** Madam?

**Cornerstone:** Oh, it's you, Duvalle?

**Cornerstone:** I'm afraid I won't be handling your make-up assignment. You'll have to ask those old gentlemen in the office when applications open.

**Duvalle:** No, Professor. I was just curious: What do you think the buildings of the future will look like?

**Cornerstone:** Me?

The architect, well accustomed to wandering through flights of fancy, adjusts her glasses and thinks for a moment.

**Cornerstone:** I don't know.

**Cornerstone:** I know what you're thinking. I'm an expert in architecture and urban planning with plenty of independent projects to my name, so I've got to know the answer.

**Cornerstone:** But even so, a city is made of people, and the way it grows cannot be separated from the choices people make for themselves.

**Cornerstone:** Perhaps a brand-new material will appear in the future, and people might choose this playful-looking style to build with it. It only takes thirty years for people to go from astonishment to acceptance.

**Cornerstone:** Of course, it's also possible that thirty years from now, people will still think Brutalism is terribly fashionable. Who can say?

**Duvalle:** Thank you ever so much, madam.

**Duvalle:** One more thing—

**Duvalle:****Have you finished your design for the monument?**

**Cornerstone:** Huh?

The student before her and the scenery of the room dissolve away like sugar into clear water, as a hand hurls the architect onto the cold, hard floor.

**Cornerstone:** Hey! You can't take revenge on me just because you failed my class!

**Cornerstone:** Ouch!

**Grundriss:** Sleep on the sofa long enough, and one day you'll roll off it.

**Grundriss:** And for the record, I've never once failed a course.

**Cornerstone:** Oh ...

The architect rubs her aching back as she pulls herself up. As she adjusts her eyes, a familiar face emerges from the blur.

**Grundriss:** Drafts, more drafts, carbon paper, enough spilled coffee beans to form a road hazard, and a ruler you snapped in your sleep ... Don't tell me you were using these drafts as a blanket.

**Cornerstone:** They're surprisingly warm, actually.

Her colleague, evidently allergic to disorder, gathers up Cornerstone's "blanket" and begins leafing through the pages one by one.

**Grundriss:**A Proposal Expressing the "Storm" Through Silk Thread and Beads, A Cemetery of Recumbent Monuments, An Umbrella Made to Shelter from the Rain ...

**Grundriss:** There are some strong contenders here, frankly.

**Cornerstone:** But they still don't feel right.

**Grundriss:** You were commissioned to design a memorial to honor those we lost in the first "Storm" and the people who worked to help us survive. It's that simple.

**Grundriss:** Your obligation is to satisfy the Foundation's requirements, not your feelings.

**Grundriss:** Forget it. So long as the final draft is good, I don't care how much sleep you lost over it.

**Grundriss:** So, where is it?

**Cornerstone:** ...

**Cornerstone:** Well, um ...

**Grundriss:** You do remember the deadline, don't you?

**Cornerstone:** The day after tomorrow?

**Grundriss:** Yesterday.

**Grundriss:** This isn't some school project, Cornerstone. You signed a contract with a set delivery date.

**Grundriss:** Do you ever think about the people you're delaying, the ones that actually have to implement your designs?

**Grundriss:** So what's the hold-up?

**Cornerstone:** It deserves to be special, to have true meaning.

**Cornerstone:** Remember Nowa Miedź?

**Grundriss:** Yeah, I remember the dome we restored a few months ago. I'm not senile.

Cornerstone picks up a crumpled ball of paper with a dejected expression. She unfolds the rejected design and holds it in her hands.

A replica of that very dome. It carried much of the message that she wanted to express, but only to them—only to the people that went through its construction.

But each design since has come across hollow and lifeless.

**Cornerstone:** Those who died in the "Storm" and those who made great contributions to surviving it ...

**Cornerstone:** My thoughts always return to my family and to that dome. But the "Storm" is larger than that.

**Cornerstone:** There must be something missing from my perspective.

**Grundriss:** So, you mean to stay in your room and ruminate until inspiration grows lonely enough to knock on your door?

**Grundriss:** I was under the impression I got on a four-hour flight so I could review your finished work in person.

The struggling architect casts her an apologetic look. Grundriss can hardly bear it. She feigns an eye roll and turns away.

**Grundriss:** \*sigh\* I can buy you a month, but remember, I'm sticking my neck out for you.

**Cornerstone:** Really?

**Cornerstone:** What's it going to cost me?

**Grundriss:** You are quick when it matters, heh. Well, I'll have to go back to the Foundation immediately. I'll speak with the project lead and try to get as much groundwork started as I can without a finalized design.

**Grundriss:** But that means I can't go to Barcelona. The Foundation office there needs someone to deal with some "minor troubles."

**Cornerstone:** I'll handle it.

**Grundriss:** Wonderful.

There's no enthusiasm in the word, just the stony glare of a woman exasperated.

**Grundriss:** By the way, I'm due there today. The next flight leaves in an hour.

**Grundriss:** You might just be able to make it if you go now.

**Cornerstone:** A plane? I-I'm sure I could catch a train there if they don't mind me arriving a little—Never mind! I owe you!

She snatches up her coat, stuffs several pencils and drawings into her pockets, grabs the tickets and papers from Grundriss's hand, and flies out the door.

**Grundriss:** \*sigh\*

Grundriss wanders through the nearly empty, yet somehow cluttered, room. From the washroom, she catches the telltale hiss of a faucet left open for god knows how long. Too long, certainly.

She picks up a discarded draft and places it atop the mountain of paper beside her.

**Grundriss:** Hold on.

The sheer height of the stack finally catches up to her.

**Grundriss:** Did she even sleep?

## 3882002 · Burning from Both Ends

<!-- reader-body:start -->
Distant, professional, meticulous.

The Foundation staff member before Cornerstone matches her initial impression to perfection; even the creases in her shirt seem to be intentionally parallel.

The only thing out of place seems to be the location for their meeting.

Cornerstone blinks, trying again to make sure she hasn't found herself in another dream.

Tents of every size have been pitched among the trees, while the normally restrained and efficient civil servants of the Foundation are gathered around eating canned beans.

She wishes Grundriss were here, if only to have another witness to this bizarre spectacle.

**Cornerstone:** Pardon me. Is this supposed to be some kind of team-building camping trip?

The woman before Cornerstone seems to remember that she is responsible for explaining this absurd scene, and her already glum features twist into an even more bitter expression.

**Rossella:** Ms. Grundriss, this is rather complicated.

**Cornerstone:** I'm Cornerstone.

**Rossella:** Ms. Cornerstone ...

She might just be imagining things, but the woman before her looks to be on the verge of tears.

**Cornerstone:** Ah, my apologies. I swapped assignments with Ms. Grundriss on short notice. I'll take care of the handover paperwork later.

**Cornerstone:** In any case, I'm here to help. Whatever trouble you're facing, just let me know.

**Rossella:** Thank goodness.

**Rossella:** Well, you see, about that ... As far as we know now, there are only a few places we've discovered that can shelter us through the "Storm" besides headquarters, but we still need to carry out our operations worldwide.

**Rossella:** It's been a point of contention whether the Foundation should adapt local buildings or construct dedicated offices of its own.

**Rossella:** And that's why we're here. This plot of land is meant to test the feasibility of creating our own structures.

**Cornerstone:** Well, it's humid, but the birds are singing, and even the spiders and geckos seem to enjoy it here. I guess it's as fine a place as any.

**Rossella:** Most importantly, it's close to the airport, which means we can withdraw to headquarters as quickly as possible before the next "Storm."

**Rossella:** Location is all that matters.

She stresses the words precisely.

**Cornerstone:** About what you said regarding building beyond headquarters ...

**Rossella:** As far as we know, it's the only place in the world that is confirmed as a "Point Irreversible."

Cornerstone swallows the words on the tip of her tongue.

Even within the Foundation, the actual number and locations of "Points Irreversible" are confidential and not widely disclosed.

**Rossella:** Since the "Storm" could arrive at any time, we're planning on using a prefab panel structure that can be finished in under two weeks.

**Cornerstone:** Oh, I know this kind of building very well. I spent my childhood in a neighborhood full of them. They have a more familiar name back home—apartment block.

**Rossella:** That's right.

**Cornerstone:** Alright. In that case, I think I more or less know what I'm here to do.

**Cornerstone:** Shall I assume that there's an issue with the building, then? Has to be something hazardous to explain this impromptu camping trip.

**Cornerstone:** Why didn't you put your people up in a hotel for now? Seems cruel to have them stuck out here with all these spiders.

**Rossella:** There isn't a hotel that can accommodate our needs within a safe range of the airport.

**Cornerstone:** Ahem ...

**Cornerstone:** Checking for hidden hazards and inspecting stress patterns ... That is indeed my colleague's area of expertise.

**Cornerstone:** Still, no need to worry. I'm just as qualified for this kind of work.

**Rossella:** Um, Ms. Grundriss—

**Cornerstone:** I'm Cornerstone ...

**Rossella:** Right, sorry. Actually, there's nothing wrong with the office itself.

**Cornerstone:** Huh?

**Rossella:** Do you know much about Shades? Poltergeists, apparitions, whatever else you prefer to call them.

**Cornerstone:** I happen to have a few Shade friends, actually.

**Rossella:** Oh, that's good to hear.

**Rossella:** We asked headquarters to send an investigator familiar with Shades and qualified as an architect to help us out with this strange situation.

**Rossella:** They recommended Ms. Grundriss.

**Cornerstone:** But I don't understand why you'd need an architect who knows about Shades.

**Cornerstone:** Surely the Foundation has plenty of arcanists who can deal with spirits. Shouldn't this be their department?

**Rossella:** That's true. In fact, when the poltergeist activity first started, we consulted the Supernatural Incidents Emergency Manual and contacted the nearest available exorcists.

**Rossella:** But each of them was picked up by the collar and tossed out of the building.

**Cornerstone:** Did any of them bother to explain why?

**Rossella:** There was one thing.

**Rossella:** They claimed there was a powerful Shade within. It demanded that they "bring someone who knows how to build a house."

**Cornerstone:** Ah, I may have misread the assignment at first, but not to worry. This is well within my area of expertise.

**Cornerstone:** I have plenty of experience dealing with mischievous spirits like this, the ill-intentioned sort that likes eating erasers and draft paper. Most of them are no trouble at all, like spoiled kids who want things a particular way.

**Rossella:** That is reassuring, Ms. Grundriss.

**Cornerstone:** I'm Cornerstone.

**Cornerstone:** By the way, have you prepared a tent for me?

**Rossella:** Uhh ...

**Cornerstone:** Or coffee? Anything that can substitute for sleep will do.

**Cornerstone:** I could really use a rest. I've gone a few days without sleep, and my heart is knocking against my rib cage, muttering something like, "Warning: cardiac arrest imminent."

**Rossella:** Oh my goodness! But you look completely fine. How incredible.

**Cornerstone:** Thank you. Looking fresh and radiant after a week of sleep deprivation is a compulsory course for architectural designers.

**Cornerstone:** A designer who looks weak and worn out can't hold her ground.

**Rossella:** I understand. Take the path on your right to Tent Seven. It's already set up, but the person using it has gone back to headquarters for the time being.

**Rossella:** Have some rest. The investigation can wait until tomorrow.

**Cornerstone:** How thoughtful of you.

**Cornerstone:** Ah, forgive me. I really do need some sleep.

**Rossella:** We'll reconvene in eleven hours.

**Rossella:** Rest well, Ms. Grundriss.

**Cornerstone:** I'm ... \*sigh\* Forget it.

**Cornerstone:** Good night.

**Duvalle:** You never told us much about your own designs, madam. I've never actually seen any of them.

**Duvalle:** In the "Storm," things that were new, things that should have come into being, things meant for the future ...

**Duvalle:** They were lost as well.

**Duvalle:** Like a twig burning from both ends.

**Rossella:** But I haven't given up hope on our project here.

## 3882004 · An Architectural Paradise

<!-- reader-body:start -->
**Cornerstone:** "What is architecture?"

**Cornerstone:** I imagine about half the students would answer, "Drawing pretty houses." Unfortunately, I thought the same thing back then.

**Cornerstone:** Students who have read biographies of legendary architects might say that it requires talent, genius, inspiration, and a good head for aesthetics, but those airy words mean very little when it comes to the fundamentals of building a safe, code-compliant modern building.

**Cornerstone:** So, what do you think architecture is?

**"Building":** You're asking me?

**"Building":** I'm just a building.

**Cornerstone:** Fair enough. Let me think about how to put this.

Cornerstone takes a fountain pen from her pocket and begins tapping it against the control console crowded with instruments and knobs.

**Cornerstone:** All buildings are structures created to fulfill a need.

**Grundriss:** You know, I often feel as if there's a screw loose in your head. But then, we are arcanists, so there's bound to be something rattling up in my head too.

Grundriss shakes her head and tosses Cornerstone the document she has been gripping for some time.

**Grundriss:** You know there's nothing left for you in Barcelona.

**Grundriss:** Not your building friend, nor the Barcelona chair that haunts your dreams.

**Cornerstone:** Even so, I'm still going.

**Cornerstone:** Don't pretend that comes as a shock.

**Grundriss:** I knew it.

A second sigh follows. As Cornerstone leaves, Grundriss is left alone in the room once more.

**Grundriss:** Everything will get back on track, won't it?

**Cornerstone:** I heard how people died in this "Storm."

**Cornerstone:** Their arms turned into linked rings, and their heads fell to the floor, becoming spheres as round and smooth as the Earth itself.

**Cornerstone:** What would you have become?

**Cornerstone:** A triangle on top of a rectangle with a little grid-like pattern drawn on it? Perhaps a small chimney too.

**Cornerstone:** Like those pictures every child has drawn at some point.

**Cornerstone:** Little kids trying to draw home.

Cornerstone walks through the overgrown woodland. The drastic change in the environment means she can only rely on the coordinates provided by the Foundation to confirm where her friend once stood.

**Cornerstone:** This really is a terrible location. I can't imagine how much those construction crews suffered getting in here.

**Cornerstone:** Well, it's humid, but the birds are singing, and even the spiders and geckos seem to enjoy it here. I guess it's as fine a place as any.

Cornerstone usually meets her clients in a custom-made suit. In social settings, an expensive appearance is often a form of hard currency.

But none of that has any real meaning to an architect.

So, she simply lies down on the grass, letting the soil stain every expensive fabric on her body.

And then she imagines.

She imagines a hard foundation breaking through her skin, gripping the soil tightly like roots.

She imagines her body swelling, her hands becoming extensions of sight as she looks out at enormous things like herself.

The airport in the distance is no longer just a series of buildings but another living thing lying beside her, their boundaries drawn on draft paper but not felt in the heart.

She imagines herself becoming a building.
