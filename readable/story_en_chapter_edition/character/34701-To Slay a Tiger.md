## 3470101 · To Slay a Tiger

> **Title Card:** Cheng's House, Ancient Zhu County

The cold sky presses down on black tiles. Black tiles press down on white walls.

At the foot of these walls is a courtyard. Though swept clean that morning, a light dusting of snow has already begun to reclaim it.

Chéng Héguāng carries firewood into the house and lights the stove.

**Cheng Heguang**: Copying documents at the Yamen still earns me around 30 wen a day.

He takes the ledger and inkstone down from a nearby shelf, dips his brush in ink, and carefully records the day's income and expenses.

**Cheng Heguang**: One bass sold for seven wen.

**Cheng Heguang**: Expenses: leaf celery, four wen. One jin of salt, five wen. Medicinal herbs, ten wen.

**Cheng Heguang**: Hm?

Someone steps lightly behind him and drapes a coat over his shoulders.

**Mother**: Back already? *cough* My, every winter feels colder than the last.

**Cheng Heguang**: You should be in bed. I only went to see Doctor Wú for some Baibu; he said you should be resting.

**Mother**: Alright, alright. But why did you go to him again? Don't you usually go to Jishitang?

**Cheng Heguang**: I wanted a new prescription. I heard he'd just made a new batch of Dazao pills.

**Mother**: Did you get some?

**Cheng Heguang**: ...

**Mother**: *sigh* I told you not to go there! In fact, I said we should've cut ties with him long ago.

**Mother**: Started up with his usual nonsense, did he? Refusing to let you inside, using some "illness" as an excuse? *cough, cough*

Chéng pauses for a moment and closes the ledger.

**Cheng Heguang**: It's no matter. I have the medicine. I'll start brewing it now.

**Mother**: What did he say?

**Cheng Heguang**: ...

At the doorway, the branches of a Mei tree shake off a few clumps of snow.

Thankfully for him, a sudden knock at the door interrupts the awkward silence.

**Mother**: Expecting someone?

**Mother**: We haven't had a visitor since your uncle returned to the countryside.

Chéng puts the ledger back on the shelf.

**Cheng Heguang**: I'll take a look.

Chéng trudges back through the snow to the courtyard gate and raises the latch.

A familiar military officer stands outside.

**Cheng Heguang**: Commander Lì?

**Li Huifeng**: Hmm. Héguāng, I hope I'm not intruding.

**Cheng Heguang**: Not at all. I just returned from the clinic and was preparing to brew some medicine.

**Li Huifeng**: How is your mother these days?

**Cheng Heguang**: Consumption is not easy to treat.

**Cheng Heguang**: Are you here on official Yamen business?

Lì Huìfēng offers a faint, amiable smile.

**Li Huifeng**: The Yamen's affairs have nothing to do with me.

**Li Huifeng**: I'm here to offer you an opportunity.

**Cheng Heguang**: ...!

Chéng returns indoors, clutching a yellowed sheet of paper.

The lid of the pot on the stove has already been lifted, the cooked rice porridge inside sending steady curls of steam into the air.

Out of habit, he takes the ladle from his mother's hand.

**Cheng Heguang**: Let me take care of this.

**Mother**: Who was it at the door, then?

**Cheng Heguang**: It was Commander Lì.

Chéng brings the porridge to the wooden table and casually passes the paper to his mother.

**Cheng Heguang**: Tomorrow, I'll ask if Auntie can come lend her hand for a while.

**Cheng Heguang**: It will be a long journey.

A few sparrows flit past the window, chirping as they flutter out of the courtyard.

> **Title Card:** Street, Riverside Village

This small village, governed by another county of Huanzhou Prefecture and nestled between mountains and rivers, has simply come to be called the Riverside Village.

This Riverside Village looks much like Zhu County. At its entrance, Chéng Héguāng sees the familiar rise and fall of black tiles once again.

Only, today's early afternoon is not quite as peaceful as usual.

**Lead Villager**: My brother's been mauled by a tiger! He's damn near on his deathbed! And you dogs have the gall to stand here, pushing us around?!

**Cheng Heguang**: ...?

To the left of the village entrance stands an exceptionally grand courtyard, with a memorial Paifang outside bearing the words "Loyalty, Filial Piety, Integrity, and Righteousness."

A group of villagers have gathered beneath the Paifang, their faces flushed as they shout at the household guards blocking their way.

**Cheng Heguang**: Likely the residence of a local gentry.

Chéng approaches without a word, slipping in among several bystanders.

**Stern Guard**: Doctor Xiù was paid for with the family's hard-earned silver! She's inside treating Master Hú. How dare you try to barge in!

**Lead Villager**: Doctor Xiù is the village physician, not the Hú family's private attendant!

**Lead Villager**: What could be more urgent than a life-or-death situation?! What right do you have to stop us? What are you hiding in there?

**Lead Villager**: Tell Doctor Xiù there's a patient out here on the verge of death! I know she'll come out to save him!

**Stern Guard**: You'll just have to find another doctor. If you set off now for the county, you can make it there before nightfall.

**Lead Villager**: We already hauled him all the way down the mountain to Doctor Xiù's clinic, only to be told she was here!

**Lead Villager**: Just look at him! Does he look as though he'll survive a trip to the county?!

The injured villager is slumped over his companion's back, his hastily bandaged left arm soaked with blood.

**Stern Guard**: That's none of our concern. We had no hand in his injuries.

**Stern Guard**: If you continue causing a scene outside Master Hú's house, we'll call the bailiff over to have you arrested!

The lead villager explodes in fury.

**Lead Villager**: Go on, call him! You're all in cahoots with one another anyway!

**Lead Villager**: Hú Hǎiyuè is so afraid of dying. He summons a doctor every time he nicks his skin.

**Stern Guard**: Watch your mouth, you slanderer!

**Lead Villager**: Our man's been mauled half to death, and you won't even let him through the door!

**Calm Villager**: They waited until land prices were low and took two acres of my land in exchange for just one tael of silver.

**Disheartened Villager**: Last year, I had a funeral to arrange and was desperate for money. The Hú family loaned it to me but charged five percent interest.

The lead villager snatches a bow from his companion's hands and aims it at the household guards. A wave of shock ripples through them.

**Lead Villager**: The Hú family's out to ruin us all! First they snatch up our fields, then they hound us with debt! They're driving us to death!

**Lead Villager**: Fellow villagers, tell me, does this sound fair to you?!

**Villagers**: No!

The rest of the villagers shout, their hoes and clubs raised high.

**Lead Villager**: We're done waiting! We're not taking it anymore!

**Lead Villager**: One way or another, I am going through that door!

**Villagers**: We'll not be pushed around! Burn down the gate! We'll force our way in!

The household guards draw their swords, raising their shields into a solid wall.

**Stern Guard**: Go ahead and try! This is nothing short of rebellion.

Before the words even finish leaving the guard's mouth, an arrow flies toward his forehead.

But before it can strike, it is cleaved in two by a flash of steel.

Chéng sheaths his blade, stepping between the guards and the villagers.

**Cheng Heguang**: This man is dying! What harm is there in letting him inside?

**Stern Guard**: Who are you?!

**Calm Villager**: He's not one of us.

The crowd exchanges glances like a theater troupe interrupted mid-performance, unsure of what to do next.

**Stern Guard**: You—

The gate is suddenly thrown open.

**???**: What is going on out there?!

## 3470102 · To Slay a Tiger

### Segment 1 `script 313702`

Calm, unhurried footsteps emerge from the courtyard.

**Cheng Heguang**: Seems they've had martial arts training.

The guards hastily make way, their voices softening with deference.

**Stern Guard**: D-Doctor Xiù, have you finished examining Master Hú?

**Doctor Xiu**: You mean his night blindness? Exactly how much time do you expect me to waste on that man?

**Doctor Xiu**: And you, out here making a racket and swinging your weapons about while I'm trying to write a prescription!

**Doctor Xiu**: Care to explain yourselves? Hm?

She takes the steps two at a time, passing Chéng without so much as a glance.

The villagers all lower their weapons, allowing her to approach the gravely injured man. She narrows her eyes as she examines him.

**Doctor Xiu**: How did this happen?

**Disheartened Villager**: We were hunting in the mountains and ran into a tiger. My brother, he fell behind and—

Before he can finish, Doctor Xiù turns and strides back toward the gate.

**Doctor Xiu**: Get him in here!

**Stern Guard**: But Doctor Xiù! He's covered in blood! How could we possibly carry him into Master Hú's courtyard?!

**Doctor Xiu**: It's bad luck for someone to die at the gate, is it not? I'll ask Master Hú for a room myself!

She pauses, then turns back impatiently.

**Doctor Xiu**: Well? Unless you wish for this man to die, I suggest you bring him in!

**Lead Villager**: Ah, r-right.

**Lead Villager**: Quickly, let's get him inside!

Two villagers rush the wounded man inside, streaking the entrance steps with blood as they ascend.

**Cowardly Guard**: Should we wipe it up? If it dries, it'll be harder to clean.

**Stern Guard**: Of course, you dolt! Unless you're looking for a scolding, you'll wipe it this instant!

The guards fetch water from the courtyard and begin hurriedly scrubbing Master Hú's steps.

**Lead Villager**: Hmph. Despicable!

**Cheng Heguang**: All's well that ends well, it seems.

Chéng is just about to leave when the lead villager steps in to block his path.

**Lead Villager**: Wait.

**Lead Villager**: Who are you? Why did you cut down my arrow?

**Cheng Heguang**: Only a passing traveler. Your arrow would never have pierced that guard's rattan shield, but it would have given them a reason to retaliate.

**Cheng Heguang**: Your recklessness will cause nothing but trouble.

**Lead Villager**: I may have lost my temper, but I've put up with them and their shoddy treatment for far too long to care about the consequences! If they fight, then we fight!

**Cheng Heguang**: And if you'd made the first move, you'd have been deemed the primary culprit. Were someone to die, your punishment would be death.

**Lead Villager**: And what of it?! You think I fear death?! Hah!

**Lead Villager**: The way you speak—it's obvious you come from noble stock. How could someone like you understand our struggles?

The disjointed back-and-forth frays Chéng's patience.

**Cheng Heguang**: I'm not—

During their exchange, several village guards rush over, stirring up a cloud of yellow dust under the Paifang.

**Headman**: What's going on here?!

Master Hú's personal guards hurry forward to greet them, directing their accusations at the villagers sitting cross-legged by the gate.

**Stern Guard**: It's these troublemakers! They've dirtied Master Hú's steps!

**Calm Villager**: We didn't mean to cause trouble. Someone was mauled by a tiger and urgently needed treatment!

**Headman**: Another tiger attack? We've lost three people this month to those beasts!

**Headman**: We have warned you over and over not to travel into the mountains. Why go up there?

**Lead Villager**: With this year's feeble harvest, what choice did we have? Five years ago it was floods, and now it's drought! Grain has grown more expensive than gold!

**Lead Villager**: If we don't hunt, what are we to eat?

**Calm Villager**: My land has all been taken, and we have no grain stored for the winter. Hunting rabbits is the only way we can scrape together a half-decent meal.

**Disheartened Villager**: If only ... If only an immortal would appear again, as they did five years ago, handing out grain to us all.

After a moment's hesitation, Chéng steps past the village guards and offers the headman a modest bow.

**Cheng Heguang**: I've had some formal weapons training, including hand-to-hand. If you aim to head into the mountains to hunt the tiger, I will offer my assistance.

The village guards exchange amused glances.

**Headman**: That mountain forest isn't meant for travel. Only a fool would go there without a good reason. Had these hunters not been so greedy, this wouldn't have happened.

**Headman**: The tiger attacked them in the forest, not the village. We can't go traipsing into the forest hunting tigers. We'd never get any real work done!

With a dismissive wave, he turns back to scold the grime-covered villagers.

**Headman**: That's enough! Go back to your homes! And no one is to go up the mountain again!

**Cheng Heguang**: Your decree doesn't change the fact that these hunters still have families to feed.

**Cheng Heguang**: That tiger has taken too many lives. It cannot be ignored.

The headman suddenly swings his staff as if he were swatting at an annoying mosquito. Its tip brushes inches from the hem of Chéng's clothes.

**Headman**: You're getting on my last nerve, boy!

**Headman**: You are neither an official nor a member of this village. What good does it serve you to stick your nose into our business?

**Headman**: If you're so eager to play the hero, go deal with the tiger yourself.

Broken cries echo from the courtyard. Chéng tightens his grip on the hilt of his blade.

**Cheng Heguang**: This is absurd.

He pushes past the guards, once again placing himself between them and the villagers.

**Headman**: Hey! What do you think you're doing?!

**Cheng Heguang**: The tiger. Can you tell me exactly where you encountered it?

> **Title Card:** Mountaintop, Riverside Village

**Cheng Heguang**: Enter the forest and head due south. After passing another slope, it is somewhere near the edge of the cliff.

**Cheng Heguang**: They said the tiger is blue and white, two or three times larger than an ordinary one. It almost sounds like a monster.

Ahead stretches a thick pine forest with shrubs reaching up to the knee. Wild grass lies flattened in the undergrowth, streaked with dull, rust-red blood.

**Cheng Heguang**: This is where the hunter was wounded. I should proceed with caution.

The forest wind falls silent, and the wild grass rustles softly.

The sound of heavy breathing reaches Chéng's ears.

**Cheng Heguang**: It's sneaked behind me!

A massive tiger, blue streaks in its magnificent white fur, bursts from the grass, slamming Chéng to the ground.

He braces his half-drawn blade across his chest, barely holding off the attack.

Blue flames flare across the tiger's brow as it presses its claws against the blade.

**Tiger**: *roar*

**Cheng Heguang**: That's some strength, hah!

A feather shoots from his shoulder guard like an arrow, piercing the tiger's forehead.

**Tiger**: Graaargh!

A thunderous roar shakes the forest. In an attempt to escape, the tiger clamps its jaws around Chéng's blade and hauls him forward.

Chéng refuses to let go as the tiger barrels forward, rushing them both to a nearby cliff edge.

**Cheng Heguang**: No!

As the tiger leaps, a sudden sense of weightlessness comes over Chéng.

In a split second, he grabs hold of the cliff edge, his body dangling below.

**Cheng Heguang**: Urgh!

### Segment 2 `script 313703`

One, two, three ... Feather after feather pierces downward.

With the final impact, the tiger's grip loosens. It lets out a final howl as it plummets down into the ravine below and vanishes out of sight.

**Cheng Heguang**: Phew!

**Cheng Heguang**: The soil is too loose. It needs to be harder if I'm to pull myself up.

**Cheng Heguang**: Breathe. Sense the flow of Qi. Wherever it touches, the earth hardens like iron.

**Cheng Heguang**: Concentrate ...

The soil beneath his fingers takes on a faint metallic sheen but doesn't spread any further.

**Cheng Heguang**: Is it too much for me to handle?

**Cheng Heguang**: ...!

As the soil's binding begins to fail, the clump in his grip begins to break apart.

A pale fog blankets the foot of the cliff. Falling would surely mean his demise.

Even so, he feels his grip beginning to falter.

**Cheng Heguang**: Darn it!

A heartbeat before his fingers give out, a thin silver thread slices through the air from the cliff edge.

**Cheng Heguang**: That's—!

## 3470103 · To Slay a Tiger

> **Title Card:** Mountaintop, Riverside Village

The silver thread coils around Chéng's waist and drags him back onto the cliff.

He hits the ground and rolls, the stabbing pain in his left leg catching up to him on impact.

A broken branch is lodged in his calf.

The village doctor stands over him, calmly reeling the silver thread back onto a bone shuttle.

**Cheng Heguang**: It's you!

**Doctor Xiu**: Playing the lone hero isn't a healthy habit.

**Cheng Heguang**: It's how I usually get things done.

**Cheng Heguang**: Someone had to resolve the matter. Agh. Besides, once I see a problem, I cannot turn a blind eye to it.

**Cheng Heguang**: I couldn't involve those hunters again, not after they scarcely made it back alive.

**Doctor Xiu**: How can you be so sure they wouldn't have lent a hand?

Doctor Xiù steps closer, squatting down and inspecting the wound on his left leg.

**Doctor Xiu**: Good news: it doesn't appear too deep.

**Doctor Xiu**: Are you ready?

She doesn't wait for an answer. In one swift motion, she yanks the branch free, and the wound wells with blood.

**Cheng Heguang**: Tch.

Satisfied there are no splinters left in the wound, Doctor Xiù guides her silver thread through a series of precise sutures.

With practiced ease, she applies herbs pulled from her robes to the wound, then cuts and ties a clean white bandage.

**Cheng Heguang**: Thank you. You've quite the unique method for treating wounds.

**Doctor Xiu**: Ah, I just find some joy in experimenting with my arcane skill.

**Cheng Heguang**: ...

**Cheng Heguang**: Forgive my bluntness, but what are you doing here, Doctor?

**Doctor Xiu**: The Hú family refused to let the hunter stay after I patched him up, so I had him taken to my clinic. Whether he survives or not is in fate's hands now.

**Doctor Xiu**: In any case, it struck me that you looked somewhat familiar.

**Doctor Xiu**: I asked around where you'd gone and was promptly informed that you'd inquired about the tiger's whereabouts and run off alone. To face the beast, no doubt.

**Doctor Xiu**: "That can't be!" I told them, but their faces were deadly serious. I was worried something might happen, so I hurried over.

Doctor Xiù falls silent, her eyes lingering on Chéng.

**Doctor Xiu**: If not for your lack of familiarity with the terrain, your getting lost notwithstanding, I doubt I'd have been able to catch up to you in time.

**Cheng Heguang**: It wasn't all that dangerous, although the tiger was fiercer than I had anticipated.

**Doctor Xiu**: Hah, the recklessness of youth.

**Doctor Xiu**: May I know your name?

**Cheng Heguang**: Chéng Héguāng.

**Doctor Xiu**: I must say, Chéng Héguāng, that flying-feather technique of yours is quite curious. A skill passed down through your family, I presume?

**Cheng Heguang**: That's right.

Doctor Xiù's winding of the bandage falters for an instant before she finishes up and knots it.

**Doctor Xiu**: I once knew a man by the name of Chéng. He commanded flying metal shards through the air. His abilities were quite impressive.

**Doctor Xiu**: Still, those who excel in such things tend to have weaker constitutions.

**Doctor Xiu**: He used to be on the wrong side of the law, but "official" was written into both his name and his fate. And sure enough, in the end, he became one.

**Doctor Xiu**: Did you know him?

Chéng clenches his teeth, his focus shifting to the remaining flying feathers on his shoulder.

**Cheng Heguang**: I can see you've discerned it already. He was my father.

Finished with the bandaging, Doctor Xiù stands up and dusts off her hands.

**Doctor Xiu**: Oh! Well, that's quite the coincidence!

**Doctor Xiu**: Your father and I were friends. Which, I suppose, makes you my nephew, or near enough.

**Doctor Xiu**: So listen, nephew: this injury will take a few days to heal, and the dressing will need changing. My clinic is at the foot of the mountain. Come stay with me for a few days.

**Cheng Heguang**: Thank you.

**Cheng Heguang**: I brought little money with me. I'm afraid I may not have enough to cover the medical expenses for the next few days.

**Doctor Xiu**: You think I'm after a few coins? Just stay. You can pay me back by lending me your hand when you can.

**Doctor Xiu**: Might I lend you mine in getting up?

**Cheng Heguang**: No need. I can manage.

**Doctor Xiu**: The two set off together down the mountain.

**Doctor Xiu**: You seem young. Are you still studying, or have you already become an official?

**Cheng Heguang**: Neither. I mostly get by on odd jobs.

**Doctor Xiu**: And what "odd job" brought you to this poor place in the middle of nowhere?

**Cheng Heguang**: I was running an errand for someone and happened to pass through.

**Doctor Xiu**: I see. How is your mother?

**Cheng Heguang**: Well enough.

**Doctor Xiu**: Hmm. And your father?

**Cheng Heguang**: He passed away.

**Doctor Xiu**: What happened?

**Cheng Heguang**: He had an accident.

**Doctor Xiu**: I'm sorry. I never would have imagined.

**Cheng Heguang**: Imagined?

**Doctor Xiu**: Mhm. He was still young. But, I suppose death comes for us all.

A long silence falls.

> **Title Card:** Foot of Mountain, Riverside Village

Reaching the foot of the mountain, they push through the grass and follow the stream for a short distance.

From between the trees, a solitary white house emerges into view.

**Cheng Heguang**: Why set up a clinic out here in the wilderness? It would be easier to see patients if you lived in the village.

**Doctor Xiu**: Convenient, sure. But the houses in the village are tall and crammed together one after another. Living like that would be maddening.

**Cheng Heguang**: But your clinic has the same black roof tiles and white walls. It looks no different from the houses in Zhu County or the village.

**Doctor Xiu**: What do you expect me to do, paint the house black from top to bottom?

Doctor Xiù whirls, sending three silver threads like spider silk toward Chéng's vital joints.

As if he'd anticipated the attack, Chéng dodges. Flying feathers clash with silver threads, tangling as they fall to the ground.

**Cheng Heguang**: What was that?!

**Doctor Xiu**: Ah, you could have stood and taken the threads, yet you chose to dodge and tear the wound open instead.

**Cheng Heguang**: ...?

**Doctor Xiu**: Your command over your arcane skill is undisciplined, to say the least. Had you used it properly earlier, you'd have subdued the tiger without so much as a scratch.

Doctor Xiù shakes her head and sighs.

**Doctor Xiu**: I remember how your father would turn leaves to metal, sending them flying as he bid. There are plenty of leaves here. Why not give it a try?

**Cheng Heguang**: ...

**Cheng Heguang**: I can't.

**Cheng Heguang**: Everyone from my clan can control light metals, but transmutation ... I am the only one left who can do it. Or at least, I ought to be.

**Doctor Xiu**: You've chosen not to use this unique ability?

**Cheng Heguang**: Not exactly.

**Cheng Heguang**: I can no longer control it, as if something is blocking me. As a child, I used it recklessly. It caused an accident. After that, with no one to guide me, I dared not use it often.

**Doctor Xiu**: A waste, if you ask me. Only you possess this ability, and yet you shackle yourself with hesitation.

**Doctor Xiu**: Your father and I sparred many times, enough for me to become familiar with his arcane skill.

**Doctor Xiu**: His arcane skill, like mine, relied on guiding matter with Qi. If you wish it, I can teach you a few techniques. Be a guide of sorts.

**Cheng Heguang**: But why?

**Doctor Xiu**: Why? Because I want to, and because it's the right thing to do.

**Cheng Heguang**: But ...

**Doctor Xiu**: Chéng Héguāng, when an elder offers you her teachings, you shut your mouth and listen.

> **Title Card:** Main Room, Clinic

Doctor Xiù swings open the clinic door without ceremony.

Inside the clinic, an assistant is busy boiling medicine. Two or three patients lie resting nearby, including the hunter whose arm was mauled earlier.

**Assistant**: Doctor, you're back! Who's this?

**Doctor Xiu**: A patient I picked up on the road. How's the hunter?

**Assistant**: He's still unconscious.

**Doctor Xiu**: Alright. Be sure to change his dressing in a couple of hours and keep a close eye on him tonight. A fever could prove fatal.

One of the patients, a young man, hops out of bed and runs over, his face flushed with color.

**Young Man**: Doctor! That medicine's incredible! I've never felt so alive, like I could plow two acres of land without so much as a breath!

**Doctor Xiu**: I'm glad to hear—

**Old Man**: Doctor! Help! Help!

Doctor Xiù lets out a weary sigh and walks over to the old man's bed.

**Doctor Xiu**: What is it this time?

**Old Man**: I still feel terribly ill. My stomach hurts, and I throw up every time I eat.

**Doctor Xiu**: I gave you strict instructions not to have any food! But you just couldn't help yourself, could you?

**Assistant**: Uh. I've just brewed him some fresh medicine. I only poured it a moment ago. It's still hot.

**Doctor Xiu**: To the assistant's surprise, Doctor Xiù smiles and looks up at Chéng, who is still standing by the door.

**Doctor Xiu**: Perfect. We have just the man for handling a little heat.

**Cheng Heguang**: ...?

## 3470104 · To Slay a Tiger

**Doctor Xiu**: Now, Chéng, move the bowl of medicine on the table into my hand using only your family's technique.

**Cheng Heguang**: Why?

**Doctor Xiu**: Do you often make a habit of arguing with your elders?

**Cheng Heguang**: ...

**Doctor Xiu**: Brewing and serving medicine is part of the clinic's duties. But, if you'd prefer to lie around and take advantage of my hospitality for the next few days, be my guest.

Chéng falls silent for a moment, then extends his right hand and aims it at the bowl of medicine.

**Cheng Heguang**: Urgh. Hngh!

In an instant, the medicine bowl turns black and spins forward, almost crashing into Doctor Xiù's hand.

**Doctor Xiu**: Hmm. A little excessive, but all in all, not bad for a first try.

**Doctor Xiu**: Although, it is rather heavy. I wonder why.

**Doctor Xiu**: Hahahaha!

She breaks into a cheerful laugh, then props the metal bowl upright and shows it to Chéng.

The medicinal broth inside the bowl has been transmuted along with it, becoming a solid block of metal.

**Doctor Xiu**: Fortunately, the effect wears off in time; otherwise, you'd be paying for the medicine.

**Doctor Xiu**: Have patience. Remember, a single day does not make a master.

**Old Man**: Owww!

**Cheng Heguang**: It's been a long while since I've used it so much.

**Cheng Heguang**: With a little more practice, I know I will perform it without error.

Doctor Xiù retrieves several chipped, broken bowls from around the room.

**Doctor Xiu**: Well, you're not lacking for confidence. Why not begin with these? Once you've mastered them, lighter things like flowers and leaves should be no problem at all.

**Doctor Xiu**: Your arcane skill works much like my own, simple, really. Sense the flow of Qi, carefully straighten it, and aim it at the object.

**Doctor Xiu**: And strike.

**Cheng Heguang**: Hyah!

The metal bowl slams into the wall once again, and Chéng's brow knots even tighter.

**Hunter**: Young man, I have watched you throw that broken bowl around day after day, and I still can't fathom why Doctor Xiù is making you do it.

Having woken up with little else to occupy him, the tiger-bitten hunter has decided to strike up a conversation with Chéng.

**Cheng Heguang**: I'm training my arcane skill.

Unable to resist, the remaining two bedridden patients chime in as well.

**Old Man**: Training? That old lady can't even throw a punch. What, does she think she's some sort of martial arts master now?

**Cheng Heguang**: What do you mean "can't throw a punch"?

**Young Man**: Haha! Did she catch you off guard too?

**Young Man**: She looks fierce, has a bad temper, and talks loud enough to rattle the bedpans, but I've never once seen Doctor Xiù get into a fight with anyone.
