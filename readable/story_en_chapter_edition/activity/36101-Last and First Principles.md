## 3610101 · That Uncanny Green

> **Subtitle:** Buried deep. No echoes return.

A silent afternoon. Enigma sits at his desk, trying to picture a field of grass.

But something else always pushes its way in—something bitter, something that tastes of rust. After several failed attempts, he pulls open the drawer and looks at what's inside.

A pistol.

A Luger P08. Practical for self-defense. A precise, purely scientific creation.

**Enigma**: Greta Hofmann.

He says the name quietly.

**Enigma**: Those who aren't taken by the "Storm"—where do they go when they die?

**Enigma**: I'd like to verify it for myself.

> **Title Card:** Cemetery

**???**: ... Never afraid of sacrifice, she followed her own path ...

**???**: ... and her fearlessness and perseverance ultimately ...

**???**: Ms. Marcus.

**Marcus**: Oh, you've arrived.

The young investigator lowers her head. A moment later, she manages a slightly easier expression.

**Marcus**: It's good to see you, Ms. Semmelweis.

Semmelweis glances at the papers in Marcus's hand. They're crumpled, and near where the ink has smeared, new letters have been neatly filled in.

**Semmelweis**: Relax. Everything will go smoothly.

**Semmelweis**: Where are the others?

**Marcus**: The Foundation's memorial ceremony has already ended. It was solemn. Almost everyone came to express their condolences. They just all had to get back to their work.

**Semmelweis**: Well, at least this cemetery will always remain open.

**Semmelweis**: I was more so wondering about those colleagues you're familiar with. Ms. Kakania, for example? I thought she'd be accompanying you.

**Marcus**: She had to go through a few more rounds of inquiries, so she couldn't make it. She did send a bouquet of flowers, though.

**Marcus**: It was just like the one you brought: a beautifully arranged bouquet of white chrysanthemums.

Semmelweis nods and surveys the figures scattered around them.

**Semmelweis**: And Enigma? Where's he gone off to? Weren't they each other's only family?

**Semmelweis**: I heard he was the one who pushed for the "Hofmann Knot" name. Rather admirable, if you ask me.

**Semmelweis**: But now, at that very sister's funeral, our Mr. Adler is nowhere to be found.

**Marcus**: A few days ago, Mr. Enigma helped me sort through Madam Hofmann's possessions.

**Marcus**: Then he left all in a hurry.

**Marcus**: Later, I received word from Laplace that he'd barred the door to his office.

**Semmelweis**: Hah. So he's chosen escapism, then.

The conversation ends there.

He, the two of them, and all the rest gathered here are little more than acquaintances to one another.

Familiar only in their mourning.

**Marcus**: ...

At last, Marcus speaks again.

**Marcus**: There was a mailing list among Madam Hofmann's possessions.

**Marcus**: On the train to Vienna, she asked me a question, hoping I could "read" its answer.

**Hofmann**: "What is the best way for someone to defend themselves if they cannot use arcane skills?"

**Marcus**: I read the answer she expected. She asked because she was preparing a gift.

The words fly out of her like fired bullets.

What a rare thing, the chance to ease unbearable grief simply by sharing it.

**Marcus**: Mr. Enigma packed the list, her documents, her ID badge, and her diary into a box. Madam Hofmann's entire life ... reduced to a tiny box.

**Marcus**: Then he stopped. And he asked me—

**Enigma**: Where do you think Greta has gone?

The ink on the paper in her hand starts to run again.

**Marcus**: "Where has she gone? Heaven? Hell? Has this 'Storm' washed them away too?"

**Marcus**: "Those who aren't lost to the 'Storm'—where do they go?"

**Marcus**: Will I see her again someday? Will all of us, any of us, see her again?

**Marcus**: If only I'd known earlier that the gift she was preparing was a birthday present for him, then instead of taking care of me and celebrating my first day as a Foundation Investigator, she could have ...

**Marcus**: If only my arcane skill were more useful! If only I'd read what would happen on that stage!

**Marcus**: If only the future were written out as words on a page!

Semmelweis says nothing. She only raises her hand gently.

But before that touch can reach her, Marcus draws a deep breath and forces her "what ifs" back down.

There is nowhere to turn for answers. No point in blaming herself. What's done is done.

**Marcus**: ...

**Marcus**: I told him I hoped Madam Hofmann had gone somewhere with no "Storm" and started a new life, even if that means we'll never see her again.

**Marcus**: In the end, he said he needed to think about my answer. He hasn't left his room since.

**Marcus**: I can understand that. I don't want to be here either. But I have to, because I want people to know that they don't need to worry about me anymore.

**Marcus**: Escapism is a natural reaction to grief. Just because you don't want to face it doesn't mean you're heartless, don't you think?

She waits almost stubbornly for an answer.

Semmelweis glances toward the distant grave and smiles softly.

**Semmelweis**: Look at you saying all that without stammering. You see? There's no need to worry.

**Marcus**: ...?

**Marcus**: S-Sorry. I-I shouldn't have suddenly ...

**Semmelweis**: No, you're right. Everyone handles grief differently.

**Semmelweis**: Let's go. I'd like to hear how your training is going. I'm sure Greta would be proud of how much her little apprentice has grown.

**Marcus**: Alright, Ms. Semmelweis.

She nods solemnly.

They pass through the gathering crowd and stand before the grave.

**Marcus**: *deep breath*

She gazes at the small box in the hole. Everything suddenly feels unreal.

She desperately wishes it were unreal, just some nightmare her mind conjured under pressure.

But every book has an end.

It's time to turn the page.

**Marcus**: Madam Hofmann was an exemplary Foundation Investigator, a dependable teammate, and my dearest mentor.

**Marcus**: She never cowered in the face of sacrifice. She followed ...

A hand settles on her back. Semmelweis finishes the sentence for her.

**Semmelweis**: She followed her own path until the end ...

**Marcus**: ... pursuing the truth with a resolute heart.

**Marcus**: And, ultimately, it was her fearlessness and perseverance that brought us the knot that allows us to cross the "Storm."

That's why I have to cherish every chance to keep the story going.

**Marcus**: She inspired many, none more than myself. She gave me the courage to leave the Flannan Isles, allowing me to witness and read so many new things. So standing here, I vow ...

**Marcus**: To continue to seek what she sought, what we all seek! A world after 1999!

Because I want to see the world you believed in.

**Marcus**: I will persevere as she did. I will keep reading, keep writing, record every experience, every feeling!

**Marcus**: I ...

**Marcus**: ... will never forget what you've done for us. For me.

## 3610102 · The Spiral

> **Subtitle:** Just a little detour.

> **Title Card:** Laplace Hallway

**???**: Damn it, are you blind?!

**Foundation Staff Member**: Honestly, is there no code of conduct among you people? You're the one barreling down the hall!

**Foundation Staff Member**: Hold on, you're—

After spotting the bite mark-covered ID badge, he wisely swallows his words.

**Foundation Staff Member**: Researcher Medicine Pocket?

**Medicine Pocket**: Oh! Sweet! Looks like even the little minions at the Foundation have heard of me.

**Medicine Pocket**: I'm rushing to get a sample right now. If you're here for Ulrich, try that lab over there. If you're here for Enigma, check his office. If you're here for me, get lost! Better luck next time.

They glance past the staff member, interest suddenly piqued.

**Medicine Pocket**: Ooh, wait, let me guess! You suits can't take it anymore, so you're gonna shove a new director on us, right?

**Medicine Pocket**: Or maybe one of the oh-so-generous higher-ups has finally approved our application for a new lab?

**Medicine Pocket**: Oh, or maybe you're here to command us very obedient researchers to fix you up an ark? You know, like Noah?

**Medicine Pocket**: Well, here's an idea: how about you shelve all that and let us get back to our research on edible Picrasma extract? At worst, we'll get a taste of something sweet!

The staff member gives an awkward wave and quickens his pace, hoping to get out of this predicament as soon as possible.

**Foundation Staff Member**: That's a topic that requires a rigorous approval process and oversight from ... I-It's not within my power to decide!

**Foundation Staff Member**: I need to deliver these files to Mr. Enigma immediately. I won't take up any more of your time!

**Medicine Pocket**: Tsk.

They toss a small metal tube to the flustered staff member.

**Foundation Staff Member**: ...?

**Medicine Pocket**: Give him this, and make sure he eats it! My application's been sitting on his desk for 30 hours. Looks like the only way I'm gonna get through to him is if he experiences it for himself!

**Medicine Pocket**: If there's an emergency, just press the button on the wall. Not the red one.

Medicine Pocket doesn't wait for a response, disappearing around the next corner instead.

The staff member is left with nothing but a closed door in the distance.

**Enigma**: I see. You can follow the proper procedures to file a complaint against Researcher Medicine Pocket.

He tosses the metal tube to one side and grabs something off his desk.

A crumpled ball of paper drops neatly into the wastebasket by the staff member's feet.

**Enigma**: Their application has already been reviewed, the ever-generous assessor that I am.

**Enigma**: As for your request, based on the progress of our other projects, you can expect a response in about 69 business days.

**Foundation Staff Member**: This is an urgent matter, sir!

**Enigma**: I understand completely. It's a pity for us both that Laplace isn't a wish-granting machine.

**Enigma**: If it truly is as urgent as you say, put the documents on the pile to your left. Make sure not to touch anything else.

**Foundation Staff Member**: Then, when's the earliest—

**Enigma**: 52 business days.

**Enigma**: In principle, my department only accepts research assignments pertaining to the "Storm." And only the final results are delivered to us.

**Enigma**: The calculations and sample analyses you need will be handed off to other teams, but it's not like we have spare researchers just moseying around. And Laplace isn't the Foundation's personal calculator.

**Enigma**: If you have any objections or extenuating circumstances, you can take it up with our director.

**Enigma**: Ah, but of course, the position's still empty. I'm only standing in to manage the documents and affairs no one else cares to deal with. Looks like it'll be a tough job to find you a team.

**Enigma**: Close the door on your way out.

**Foundation Staff Member**: ...

To his surprise, the staff member doesn't argue. But he doesn't budge, either.

**Enigma**: Tch, you can move your own legs without your boss's orders, can't you? Get out.

**???**: I'm glad to see you're still full of vigor, Adler.

His head snaps up in surprise as a familiar figure steps inside.

Seeing who it is, he finally sets down his pen.

**Enigma**: Ah, if it isn't my old friend. What are you fetching for Constantine this time?

**Enigma**: I trust the last report you snatched was to your satisfaction? If anything else in these stacks of scrap paper strikes your fancy, then by all means, help yourself.

He extends an arm and pats the mountain of files beside him.

**Z**: That won't be necessary. Though, I'll be sure to visit again should the need arise.

**Z**: Mark, there are still some details regarding the proposed adjustment to field agent rations I'd like you to confirm.

**Z**: Mr. Ulrich and Ms. Simone have cleared a fifteen-minute block in their schedules. You'll find them in Meeting Room No. 3.

**Foundation Staff Member**: Very well.

The door closes.

**Enigma**: You knew of Ms. Simone's return today?

**Enigma**: Most assumed she retired to the Timekeeper's Suitcase along with Madam Lucy.

**Enigma**: Looks like the Foundation keeps as close an eye on Laplace as they do on Manus Vindictae.

He scoffs.

**Enigma**: Heh. Some might even say that mad scientists and fanatical arcanists aren't so different. Though the former does have the small advantage of providing a handy little invention here and there.

**Z**: Always thinking from a researcher's perspective, Adler.

**Z**: Every researcher wants an independent, undisturbed laboratory. A simple wish in theory, but difficult to implement in practice.

**Enigma**: Hm. I'm sure you didn't come all this way just to lecture me.

**Enigma**: What's the matter?

**Z**: I trust Ulrich has told you what transpired in Antarctica.

**Z**: Not long ago, we received reports from our field agent squads and investigators.

**Z**: This time, the "Storm" has brought us to the year 1920. And to a war that should no longer exist.

**Enigma**: The "Storm" is hardly a perfect formula, and historical records often contain inaccuracies. Even under our current theoretical models, arcanum maintains a degree of uncertainty and unknowability. A minor error such as this should come as no surprise to you.

He takes the stack of reports in Madam Z's hand nonetheless.

As he flips through its pages, his expression grows serious.

**Enigma**: Hm. An error of this magnitude doesn't follow any of our current models.

**Enigma**: This "Flood" has destroyed at least two-thirds of all our prior findings!

**Z**: Since the beginning, we've diligently tracked the occurrence frequency of the "Storm." We've also long gotten used to it abandoning any semblance of a pattern.

**Enigma**: Hah, because those who haven't "gotten used to it" are no longer with us.

A scornful laugh.

**Z**: It's never easy to have your world turned upside-down. For anyone.

**Z**: It's overwhelming at first, but the more you see it happen, the easier it is to accept. Of course, some still have their objections.

**Z**: There have been countless debates on whether we should prepare for war.

**Enigma**: Laplace is not an arsenal.

**Z**: Our more radical members believe that since research on the "Storm" will continue to be lost, we should divert our resources elsewhere.

**Z**: The LSCC has been without a director for a long time, and the arcanocentric society "Institutum Lorentz" has gone unchecked. There's reason to believe that this has impacted Laplace's research efficiency.

She studies Enigma's expression, knowing he's already figured out why she has come.

**Z**: So, to ensure our collaboration with Laplace continues to yield valuable results, the Foundation plans to dispatch a director of their own choosing.

**Enigma**: Absolutely not.

His clenched fist never comes down on the desk.

He stifles his anger. Rage won't help him now.

**Z**: I don't agree with the sentiment, nor do I believe the Foundation should assume complete control of Laplace.

**Z**: However, the reality is that—besides some perfect directing machine—the members will be the most easily persuaded if we appoint a human as director.

**Z**: Which is why I've come to you today.

**Z**: If, under your leadership, Laplace can produce a persuasive new discovery during this tumultuous period, it would help my cause immensely.

**Z**: Of course, no matter what, I'll continue to express my support for Laplace's independence to the Foundation.

Enigma looks at his old colleague. They worked together a long time ago, if only briefly.

**Enigma**: What exactly are you hoping to achieve with all this politicking? For Laplace to align itself with the Foundation, or with yourself?

**Enigma**: You don't seriously expect a group of scientists to spend their time strategizing these things like some kind of warlords pushing clay figurines around a sand table, do you?

Madam Z doesn't answer directly.

**Z**: I'd like you to continue your research on the toybox sent by Musae III. Investigate the source of its power and use its simulations to push out a report.

**Z**: Producing a final Pre-Storm Protocol will refute the accusations that the LSCC lacks risk management policies, and the core of the toybox may just provide the "new results" you need.

**Enigma**: I see your reasoning. But why are you telling me this? Surely you aren't offering your help simply out of the goodness of your heart.

**Enigma**: What exactly are your feelings toward Laplace nowadays? Nostalgia? Or some kind of scientific idealism?

Madam Z pauses. Then she brings up that old, often-discussed topic.

**Z**: I still believe we can reach the final theory, something all-encompassing that unifies the entire universe.

Enigma falters, then lets out a sigh.

**Enigma**: Well, in that, we've just been going in circles. In a decade, all we've managed is to return to the 11th dimension.

**Z**: Mm. But that doesn't mean all our past efforts have been meaningless.

She rises, cutting the conversation short.

**Z**: The Timekeeper hopes to understand the situation outside as soon as possible. Once you've arrived at a conclusion, please contact me immediately.

The door closes again.

Madam Z stands in the corridor, gazing up at the unfamiliar silver-white building.

**Z**: Can a scientist ...

**Z**: ... become a politician?

3 PM.

**Hissabeth**: Hah, sounds like you've gotten over accidentally ordering Critter chow at the canteen. I suppose it is quite difficult to decipher a Tetris-based language, no?

**Ulrich**: Adler already notified us that the Foundation is pressuring us to deliver persuasive research results!

**Ulrich**: This isn't the first time they've used our "laissez-faire attitude" as an excuse to attack Laplace. We need to use the toybox's simulations to create a reliable emergency protocol!

**Medicine Pocket**: Okay, I get you. We just need to submit something that'll shut them up.

**Medicine Pocket**: So, why don't you round up everyone who's been wasting research materials, hand them all "Lorentz" ID badges, and turn them over?

**X**: I also have a personal theory. Mr. Ludwig's arcane fluctuations ...

Thud!

**???**: Mr. Enigma.

**Enigma**: I will handle this myself. After all, I was the one who questioned Medicine Pocket on the possibility of resuming research on the topic in the first place.

**X**: It's a compelling research direction. An antidote for Picrasma extract suitable for human consumption and a universally applicable, edible extract with energizing effects.

He delivers the long string of terms with neither a stumble nor a pause, as if reciting from a script.

**X**: There's no need for you to worry. I read the project proposal. Medicine Pocket already consulted me on a few issues.

**Enigma**: That proposal was supposed to be confidential. *sigh* Never mind, but we need to act like this research topic doesn't exist until it yields some results.

**Enigma**: You are all pureblood arcanists. You have a natural tolerance for Picrasma extract. But it's simply too dangerous to move straight on to pureblood humans.

**Enigma**: Well, I suppose it's about time my quarter-arcanist blood came in handy.

**X**: I understand, Mr. Enigma. I have no intention—nor the power, mind you—to interfere with anyone's decision.

**X**: But I did receive a document I think you should see.

**Enigma**: Of course you did. There's always more.

**X**: It's about the "language disorder" caused by the toybox's malfunction. I'm sure you're familiar with it.

**X**: Although the world outside has returned to normal, a lot of curious occurrences have appeared in the toybox. Allegedly, the behavior of its inhabitants has been becoming increasingly formulaic and unified.

**X**: Of course, these are Mr. Ulrich's words. I haven't seen it for myself.

**X**: The specifics are on top.

He hands over the document.

**X**: I read the whole thing. It's comprehensive. Exhaustive, even. It should be more than enough to submit to the Foundation.

**X**: But Ulrich isn't convinced it's persuasive enough, so he hasn't turned it in.

**X**: We all know how easy it is to fall down a research rabbit hole. He needs someone on the outside to step in and make a decision.

**X**: At least, that's what I think.

**X**: There's also the small matter of a waveform contaminating the toybox's arcane fluctuations—a waveform extremely similar to that of our dear Mr. Raven.

**X**: I've compared it. It's distinctly different from the other simulation data.

X delivers the last of his prepared lines.

And with that, the butterfly has flapped its wings.

He steps out and shuts the door behind him.

**Enigma**: Ulrich!

**Ulrich**: ...

**Enigma**: I'm trying to return this copy of your report that you "forgot."
